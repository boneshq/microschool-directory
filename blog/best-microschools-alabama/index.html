Level Up School of Innovation — Prattville

Level Up School of Innovation is a learner-driven microschool in Prattville, near Montgomery, that serves grades 3 through 12. Students sort into three levels: LUSI Brights for grades 3-5, LUSI Flights for grades 6-8, and LUSI Heights for grades 9-12. Accreditation comes from the WASC Accrediting Commission for Schools.

Days run on project-based learning and real-world experiences rather than a lecture-and-test cycle. Level Up describes its mission as helping each learner explore curiosity through hands-on work, with small groups and personalized pacing.

Enrollment is open for the fall term, and Level Up School of Innovation does not post a tuition figure online, so ask the school directly for current pricing and the grade levels with open seats.

Res Novae Academy — Pinson

Res Novae Academy is a hybrid microschool in Pinson, northeast of Birmingham, that serves kindergarten through 12th grade. Students at Res Novae Academy attend in person or virtually two days a week, on Monday and Tuesday, and complete three days of guided learning at home. Accreditation comes from the WASC Accrediting Commission for Schools.

Published tuition at Res Novae Academy is $6,500 a year for grades K-8, and the school states a conservative and libertarian values approach. That two-day rhythm fits families who want classroom instruction alongside time for home-based work.

Res Novae Academy accepts the Alabama CHOOSE Act scholarship and sets enrollment deadlines of May 31 for on-site students and July 31 for virtual students. Confirm the current grade ranges and any seats left before the deadline that applies to your child.

Yes I Can Preparatory Center — Northport

Yes I Can Preparatory Center is a project-based microschool in Northport, near Tuscaloosa, that serves ages 3 to 18. Built for homeschoolers, nontraditional learners, and families who want a flexible schedule, the center keeps its age band wide on purpose. Days at Yes I Can Preparatory Center run on sensory-rich, hands-on learning experiences.

Programming spans literacy, the arts, and character building across a wide age band, which suits families with children in more than one grade. That wide age range also lets siblings attend the same program rather than splitting across schools.

No tuition figure or fixed daily schedule is published online, so contact Yes I Can Preparatory Center for current pricing, the weekly calendar, and which age groups have openings before you apply.

How much do microschools cost in Alabama?

Microschool tuition in Alabama starts around $500 a year at the low end and reaches $6,500 a year at Res Novae Academy for grades K-8, based on the published figures in our directory. Most Alabama microschools do not post tuition online, so the real cost for a given school usually requires a direct inquiry. Part-time and hybrid tracks generally cost less than full-time enrollment.

Such a wide range reflects how different these schools are. A two-day hybrid week or a part-time program sits at the low end, while a full-time, full-week microschool with a building and paid staff sits near the top.

Alabama families can offset tuition through the CHOOSE Act, the state's education savings account program run by the Alabama Department of Revenue. The program provides eligible K-12 students up to $7,000 a year toward tuition, tutoring, and other approved expenses at participating schools, and up to $2,000 for home-education students. Check current rules and application windows on the state's official page at CHOOSE Act Alabama.

Choosing a microschool in Alabama: a parent's checklist

To choose a microschool in Alabama, start with the learning model, learner-driven, hybrid, project-based, or faith-based, then check the grade range, the weekly schedule, the cost, and whether the program accepts the CHOOSE Act. Visit in person and ask how a typical day runs before you commit.

Practical steps:

  • Match the model to your child. A learner-driven school and a two-day hybrid school produce very different weeks; decide which suits your child first.
  • Verify the grade range and open seats. Some Alabama microschools start at grade 3; others run K-12 or accept ages 3 to 18. Confirm which grades have openings.
  • Pin down the real cost. Few schools post tuition, so ask for the full-time and part-time figures, plus any enrollment fee, in writing.
  • Check funding fit. Ask whether the school accepts the CHOOSE Act and how payment timing works against the state funding schedule.
  • Tour and observe. Watch a normal day, meet the staff, and ask how the school handles reading instruction, conflict, and progress reporting.

If a term like learner-driven or hybrid is new to you, our glossary of terms defines the models plainly. For a deeper walk-through of tours, questions, and warning signs, read the complete guide to microschools.

Confirm current rules and the application window on the state's official page before applying.

Are microschools in Alabama legit schools?

Microschools in Alabama operate as small private schools or hybrid homeschool programs. Several featured here, including Level Up School of Innovation and Res Novae Academy, are accredited by the WASC Accrediting Commission for Schools. We verified each featured school's website was live and described an operating program. Ask any school about its legal status, accreditation, and how it reports student progress.
