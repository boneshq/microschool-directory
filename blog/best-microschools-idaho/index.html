The best microschools in Idaho at a glance

Our three editors' picks for the best microschools in Idaho are Acton Academy Rexburg in Rexburg, a learner-driven studio for ages 4 to 14; Heroes Academy in Boise, a Pre-K through 12 learner-driven school; and Idaho Evergreen: An Acton Academy in Cascade, a Christ-centered Acton program founded in 2022. Acton Academy Rexburg — Rexburg

Acton Academy Rexburg is a learner-driven microschool that splits students into three studios by age: Spark Studio for ages 4 to 7, Discovery Studio for ages 7 to 11, and Adventure Studio for ages 11 to 14. The campus sits between Rexburg and Sugar City, east of the Teton River Temple, and the school says a new campus is coming this fall, between Rexburg and Sugar City.

The program runs on the Acton framework of "Learn to Learn," "Learn to Do," and "Learn to Be," pairing self-paced core skills with hands-on quests in science, entrepreneurship, and the arts. Students work through Socratic discussions and a hero's-journey structure rather than teacher lectures, and adaptive software handles much of the reading and math practice.

Families looking at Acton Academy Rexburg in our directory should confirm which studio fits their child's age and ask which campus their child would attend before enrolling.

Heroes Academy — Boise

Heroes Academy is a learner-driven microschool serving the Treasure Valley around Boise, with studios that span Pre-K through 12th grade. The school is accredited by the International Association of Learning Design Schools and organizes students into Discovery, Launchpad, and Adventure studios.

Learning here is self-paced and project-based: students earn points for effort and badges for mastery, run their own studio governance, and use technology for literacy and numeracy work. The wide Pre-K through 12 range is unusual for a microschool, so the daily experience differs a lot between a young Discovery learner and an older Adventure student.

See the full listing for Heroes Academy to schedule a tour. Because the school covers so many grades, confirm which studio your child would join and what a week looks like in that room.

Idaho Evergreen: An Acton Academy — Cascade

Idaho Evergreen is a Christ-centered, learner-driven microschool in Cascade that opened in 2022 and runs on the Acton Academy model. It is part of the worldwide Acton network of more than 300 campuses, and it builds its program around Socratic discussion and real-world quests rather than grades or class rankings.

The school organizes learning around the same three Acton pillars, with quests on topics such as marine science, the Revolutionary War, and architecture, plus Servant Leader Badges for character growth. Idaho Evergreen sits in a small mountain town north of Boise, which gives it a different setting from the Treasure Valley and eastern Idaho programs.

Read the Idaho Evergreen: An Acton Academy listing for contact details. Ask directly about grades served and the weekly schedule, since the site does not publish those specifics.

Our method for these Idaho microschools

We picked these three Idaho microschools for regional spread and a verified web presence, not by a numerical score. Each school appears in the microschool.directory listings, runs a working website, and sits in a different part of the state. This is an editors' pick, not a ranked league table.

Idaho is unusual: all 5 microschools we list are learner-driven, and four of the five run on the Acton Academy model. We did not force a fake mix of models that the state does not have. Instead we chose three schools that cover eastern Idaho, the Boise area, and the central mountains, so a parent can compare programs by region and age range rather than by model.

We confirmed each school's studios, model, and location against the school's own site in 2026. Any microschool can change its program between years, so treat every detail here as a starting point and verify with the school.

How much do microschools cost in Idaho?

Two of our three picks publish tuition. Acton Academy Rexburg lists $6,850 a year for ages 4 to 11, payable in 10 monthly installments, plus a $400 enrollment fee; its middle and high school pricing is marked coming soon. Heroes Academy in Boise lists $8,700 a year for Pre-K through elementary and $9,700 for middle and high school, plus a $500 enrollment fee. Idaho Evergreen does not post tuition, so contact the school. Acton-affiliated schools set their own rates locally, so ask for the full fee schedule before you enroll.

Idaho does have a funding option. The Parental Choice Tax Credit, enacted in 2025 and in effect for 2026, is a refundable tax credit of up to $5,000 per child, or up to $7,500 for a child with a qualifying disability, that families can apply to nonpublic-school and microschool costs; the Idaho Supreme Court unanimously upheld it on Feb. 5, 2026. Funding is capped at about $50 million a year and goes first to lower-income families. The 2026 application window closed Aug. 15, 2026, so watch the Idaho State Tax Commission page for the next one. Families who teach fully at home should note that Idaho does not regulate homeschooling and requires no registration; the Idaho Department of Education homeschool page explains the current rules.

How to choose a microschool in Idaho

To choose a microschool in Idaho, start by comparing the specifics rather than the model, since nearly every program here is learner-driven. Confirm the ages and studios served, the weekly schedule, and the class size, then tour in person and read the enrollment terms before paying.

Ask how guides track progress, how many students share a studio, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just convenience. Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Idaho?

microschool.directory lists 5 microschools in Idaho across 5 cities: Boise, Caldwell, Cascade, Rathdrum, and Rexburg. Every listed school follows a learner-driven model, and four of the five use the Acton Academy framework. New programs open often, so the count grows through the year.

How much does a microschool cost in Idaho?

Published rates vary. Acton Academy Rexburg lists $6,850 a year for ages 4 to 11 plus a $400 enrollment fee, and Heroes Academy in Boise lists $8,700 to $9,700 a year plus a $500 enrollment fee. Idaho Evergreen does not post tuition. Idaho's Parental Choice Tax Credit can offset part of the cost. Always confirm current pricing on the school's own site.

Does Idaho have an ESA or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Idaho has the Parental Choice Tax Credit, a refundable tax credit enacted in 2025. It provides up to $5,000 per child, or up to $7,500 for a child with a qualifying disability, toward nonpublic-school and microschool costs. The Idaho Supreme Court unanimously upheld it on Feb. 5, 2026. Funding is capped at about $50 million a year with priority for lower-income families, and the 2026 application window closed Aug. 15, 2026.

How do I choose a microschool in Idaho?

Because nearly every Idaho microschool is learner-driven, compare the specifics instead of the model. Confirm the ages and studios served, the weekly schedule, and the class size. Tour the school, ask how guides track progress, and read the enrollment terms. Our parent guide walks through the full checklist.

Are microschools in Idaho legit?

Yes. An Idaho microschool is a small private school or a homeschool collective. Idaho does not regulate homeschooling and requires no registration, so the responsibility sits with parents. Each program we feature has a working website and verified program details, but parents should still confirm references and terms directly.
