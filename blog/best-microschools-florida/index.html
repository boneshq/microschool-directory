The best microschools in Florida at a glance

Three standout microschools in Florida are Acton Academy Palm Harbor (learner-driven, Palm Harbor), River Oak Academy (Montessori-Acton blend, St. Johns), and Colossal Academy (project- and inquiry-based, Fort Lauderdale). These three are editor picks from the 166 microschools listed in our Florida directory, chosen to show a range of learning models and regions across the state.

Florida has more microschools in our directory than any other state. Listings span 97 cities, with the most schools in Tampa, Fort Lauderdale, and Miami. By model, the general microschool label accounts for 110 of the 166 listings, followed by 17 learner-driven, 13 project-based, 9 Montessori, and 6 forest or nature programs. Our three picks below sit in three of those buckets and three different metro areas.

Acton Academy Palm Harbor — Palm Harbor

Acton Academy Palm Harbor is a learner-driven microschool at 1588 E Klosterman Road that serves kindergarten through 12th grade, grouped into four studios: Spark (K-2), Discovery (3-5), Middle School (6-8), and Launchpad High School. Hours run Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and tuition for 2026-27 is $12,000, paid in monthly installments of $1,200.

Studio work leans on Socratic discussion, self-paced lessons, and real-world quests rather than lectures. Older students take on apprenticeships and entrepreneurship projects, while adults act as guides instead of front-of-room teachers.

Families who qualify can apply Step Up For Students scholarship funding at Acton Academy Palm Harbor, which offsets much of the published tuition.

River Oak Academy — St. Johns

River Oak Academy is a microschool in St. Johns that blends Montessori practice with the Acton learner-driven method. Students are grouped into Toddler, Primary, and Lower Elementary levels, called "Learners," and staff act as facilitators and guides rather than lecturers. Located at 3355 State Road 13, River Oak Academy lists an active admissions line for tours and applications.

Hands-on work is a core part of the day. Woodworking instruction is part of the program, and a Children's Business Fair has students plan and sell a product of their own.

Because River Oak Academy publishes its program detail by level, families touring the school can match a child's age to the right classroom. No flat tuition figure is posted, so confirm current pricing directly during a tour.

Colossal Academy — Fort Lauderdale

Colossal Academy is a microschool in downtown Fort Lauderdale serving grades 6 through 10. An inquiry-based, place-based model pairs academics with projects rooted in the surrounding city, and class sizes stay small. Located at 746 NE 3rd Ave, Colossal Academy was named a quarterfinalist for the VELA Education Fund Next Step Grant and the Yass STOP Award.

Place-based learning here means students study and work in their own community rather than only inside a classroom. That grade 6-10 range makes Colossal Academy a fit for middle and early high school families specifically.

Tuition is not published online for Colossal Academy, so ask the admissions office about current rates and any scholarship options during the inquiry process.

How much do microschools cost in Florida?

Microschools in Florida in our directory cluster in the $8,800 to $13,500 per year range, though many smaller programs do not publish a flat figure and quote by grade, schedule, or number of days per week. Acton Academy Palm Harbor lists $12,000 for 2026-27, which falls inside that range; River Oak Academy and Colossal Academy quote during the admissions process.

Florida funding can cut that cost sharply. The state's Family Empowerment Scholarship and the Personalized Education Program, both administered through Step Up For Students, give eligible families an education savings account that can be applied to private-school tuition and approved microschool expenses. Read the program details at the Step Up For Students site before you assume a published price is your final cost.

Eligibility for the Family Empowerment Scholarship is broad. A 2023 expansion removed the income cap, so most school-age Florida residents can apply regardless of household income, subject to available funding. Award amounts are set by the state each year and follow the student, not the school.

Because microschool pricing varies so widely, treat any single number as a starting point. Ask each school what the figure covers, whether it includes materials and field work, and which scholarships the school accepts. One program that quotes a higher base rate can still cost a family less than another once an education savings account is applied.

How to choose a microschool in Florida

To choose a microschool in Florida, start by naming what your child needs, then match a learning model to that need. Tour at least two schools, ask about group sizes and daily schedule, confirm grade range and tuition, and check which scholarships each school accepts. A microschool that fits one family can be wrong for another, so judge fit over reputation.

A few practical steps:

  • Decide on a model first. Learner-driven schools like Acton run very differently from a Montessori classroom or a project-based microschool. The glossary of terms explains each model in plain language.
  • Confirm the grade range. Colossal Academy serves grades 6-10; River Oak Academy starts at Toddler. Make sure the school covers your child's age now and for the next few years.
  • Ask about funding early. Confirm whether the school accepts Step Up For Students scholarships and how billing works alongside an education savings account.
  • Tour in person. Watch how adults talk to students and how a normal hour actually runs.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Florida?

Our directory lists 166 microschools in Florida across 97 cities. The largest concentrations are in Tampa, Fort Lauderdale, and Miami. The most common model is the general microschool, with learner-driven, project-based, and Montessori programs also represented.

How much do microschools cost in Florida?

Microschools in Florida in our directory cluster in the $8,800 to $13,500 per year range. Many smaller programs quote by grade, schedule, or days per week instead of a flat figure. Acton Academy Palm Harbor, for example, lists $12,000 for the 2026-27 year.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Florida?

Yes. Florida's Family Empowerment Scholarship and Personalized Education Program, both run through Step Up For Students, give eligible families an education savings account. Approved microschool tuition and expenses can be paid from that account. Confirm acceptance with each school before enrolling.

How do I choose a microschool in Florida?

Start by naming what your child needs, then match a learning model to that need. Tour at least two schools, ask about group sizes, daily schedule, grade range, and tuition, and check which scholarships each school accepts. Fit matters more than reputation, since a school that suits one family can be wrong for another.

Are microschools in Florida legitimate?

Yes. Microschools operate in Florida as private schools and are eligible for state scholarship funding through Step Up For Students. Each school in our directory has a working website our editors reviewed. Parents should still verify a school's registration, staff, and current program details directly during a tour.
