Sarah Beth Academy — North Bellmore

Sarah Beth Academy is a hybrid co-op in North Bellmore serving PreK through 12th grade, with a school year that runs from September to the first week of June. Its program mixes core academic instruction with co-op classes in art, science, creative writing, Spanish, geography, history, literature, coding and robotics, and gym.

This setup suits families who want structured days outside the home without a full-time private-school commitment. Students take part in hands-on projects, including a community garden at the Cornell Cooperative Extension farm in East Meadow.

Enrichment camps run during summer, winter, spring, and holiday breaks. Parents who want a year-round option, not just September through June, should ask how the camps connect to the school-year program.

HwH Academy — Melville

HwH Academy is a microschool in Melville, run by Homework Hub, serving grades 5 through 8. Classes meet Monday through Friday from 9:00 AM to 2:30 PM, with a 6:1 student-to-teacher ratio. Teachers there hold New York State certification and master's degrees.

HwH Academy pairs core academic instruction with executive-function skill work, which makes a microschool of this size a fit for middle-schoolers who do better in a small group than a large classroom. Instruction follows the Half Hollow Hills school calendar.

That small ratio and the certified staff are the two facts worth confirming on a tour, since they shape the daily experience more than any brochure language. At the time of our review, HwH Academy was enrolling for the 2026-2027 school year.

Mosaic Yonder — Clifton Park

Mosaic Yonder is an independent high school in the Town of Clifton Park, in New York's Capital Region near Albany. Mosaic Yonder runs an inquiry-based, student-centered model built around curiosity-driven, real-world projects rather than lecture-and-test instruction.

The program is organized around five stated values: respect, inclusivity, curiosity, accountability, and empathy. Among New York microschools, Mosaic Yonder is one of the few options aimed squarely at high-school-age students rather than younger grades.

As a newer school, Mosaic Yonder published a 2025-2026 calendar, so families should confirm current enrollment, grade levels, and the daily schedule directly before applying.

How much do microschools cost in New York?

Tuition in our New York listings starts around $800 per month, with full-time private microschools generally costing more than part-time co-ops. Most New York microschools publish tuition on request rather than online, so the most reliable figure is the one you confirm with each school for the current year.

New York gives microschool families no public funding help. There is no state education savings account, no private-school voucher, and no K-12 tax-credit scholarship, which means tuition is paid out of pocket. Some schools offer sliding-scale tuition or payment plans, so ask each one directly.

Cost also tracks the format. A part-time co-op that meets two or three days a week generally costs less than a full-week microschool with certified staff, because you are paying for fewer contact hours. When you compare two schools, divide the annual tuition by the number of days per week to get a clearer per-day figure.

Most New York microschools and co-ops operate under the state's home instruction rules. Families filing as home instruction submit an annual plan and quarterly reports to their local district; the New York State Education Department home instruction page lists the current requirements.

How to choose a microschool in New York

To choose a New York microschool, match the model to your child, tour in person, and verify the legal and financial details before you enroll. Compare a hybrid co-op against a full-week microschool, ask about the student-to-teacher ratio and teacher credentials, and confirm the grades served and the daily schedule.

Use these practical steps:

  • Shortlist by city and model from the microschools in New York directory.
  • Learn the model terms first; our glossary of terms defines Montessori, project-based, hybrid, and homeschool co-op.
  • Tour each school, sit in on a session, and ask about ratio, credentials, and how the school handles state reporting.
  • Confirm tuition, payment plans, and any sliding scale for the current year.
  • Check that the school meets New York's home instruction requirements or holds private-school registration.

Geography matters too. Two of our three picks are on Long Island and one is in the Capital Region, so a New York family may need to widen the search radius or weigh a commute against the model they want. One school across the county line with the right model can beat a closer one that does not fit.

If you are new to this kind of school, read our complete guide to microschools first, then come back to compare specific New York schools. A microschool is a serious commitment, and the parents who are happiest with the choice are the ones who toured, asked direct questions, and checked the paperwork before signing.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in New York?

Our directory lists 25 microschools and homeschool co-ops across New York as of 2026. The listings span 23 cities and towns, from Long Island through the Capital Region near Albany. Montessori programs and homeschool co-ops are the two most common models in the state.

How much do microschools cost in New York?

Tuition in our New York listings starts around $800 per month, and full-time private microschools often run higher than part-time co-ops. New York has no state voucher or education savings account, so most families pay tuition directly. Always confirm the current rate with each school, since fees change year to year.

Does New York have an ESA or school-choice scholarship for microschools?

No. The state offers no education savings account, no private-school voucher, and no tax-credit scholarship for K-12 tuition. Families who choose a microschool or homeschool co-op pay out of pocket. Some schools offer their own sliding-scale tuition or payment plans, so ask each one directly.

How do I choose a microschool in New York?

Start by matching the learning model to your child: hybrid co-ops fit families who want a few structured days, while full-time microschools cover the whole week. Tour in person, ask about the student-to-teacher ratio and teacher credentials, and confirm grades served and the daily schedule. Then check that the school meets New York's home instruction requirements.

Are microschools in New York legit?

Microschools operate legally in New York, most often under the state's home instruction regulations, which require families to file an annual instruction plan and quarterly reports with their local district. A microschool is not an accredited private school unless it states otherwise. Verify each school's registration and ask how it handles state reporting before you enroll.
