The best microschools in Illinois at a glance

Three of the best microschools in Illinois are Midwest Academy for Gifted Education in Chicago, FreeStyle Learning Center Microschool in Country Club Hills, and Acton Academy Barrington. They run three different kinds of small school: a gifted-focused private school, a whole-child homeschool support program, and a learner-driven Acton studio. Midwest Academy for Gifted Education — Chicago

Midwest Academy for Gifted Education is a private gifted school in Chicago that serves high-ability students from junior kindergarten through high school. The academy runs a full-day program and groups students by readiness rather than age alone, with a stated focus on identifying and teaching gifted learners.

Programs at Midwest Academy for Gifted Education span junior kindergarten and kindergarten, elementary, an Academic Center middle school, and a selective-enrollment high school track. Families unsure whether the academy is a fit can start with the school's own guidance on how to tell if a child is gifted.

Chicago parents whose child needs a faster pace or more depth than a standard classroom offers should start here. Confirm current openings by grade, since a gifted microschool fills specific seats rather than whole grade levels. See the Midwest Academy for Gifted Education listing for contact details.

FreeStyle Learning Center Microschool — Country Club Hills

FreeStyle Learning Center Microschool in Country Club Hills serves families in Chicago's south suburbs who want to homeschool but cannot teach during the workday. This microschool uses individualized tutoring built around each student's learning style and describes its approach as educating the whole child rather than running worksheets.

FreeStyle Learning Center Microschool works as a drop-off option for homeschooling parents who hold jobs or other obligations. Each student keeps a homeschool path while getting daytime instruction and structure on site. Reach the school at (708) 765-5375 for grade ranges and schedule.

A south-suburban family that has chosen homeschooling on paper but needs a place for the child to learn during the day will fit this microschool well. Ask how the center coordinates its tutoring with your at-home plan. Its FreeStyle Learning Center listing has the rest of the contact information.

Acton Academy Barrington — Barrington

Acton Academy Barrington is a learner-driven microschool in the Barrington and Palatine area that runs Socratic discussion and self-paced core work for elementary and middle-school students. This studio uses a Montessori-inspired approach for younger learners and measures progress by mastery rather than grades.

Students at Acton Academy Barrington are organized into an Elementary Studio, a Launchpad Studio, and a Middle School Studio. Acton offers a full-time on-campus option and a hybrid schedule with Monday through Wednesday on campus and the rest of the week completed at home. Students govern parts of studio life through structures such as Squads and an Excellence Committee.

Families who want a child to own daily work and learn through discussion instead of lectures will fit this Acton studio. Its hybrid option also helps parents who want fewer on-campus days. See the Acton Academy Barrington listing to reach the studio.

How much do microschools cost in Illinois?

Tuition for microschools in Illinois is not published by the three schools above, and our directory does not yet carry a verified statewide tuition range for Illinois. Nationally, microschool tuition commonly runs from a few thousand dollars to around $15,000 a year, but Illinois families should confirm price directly with each school.

Illinois currently has no statewide education savings account or school voucher, so most microschool tuition is paid out of pocket. The state's Invest in Kids tax-credit scholarship, which once helped some private-school families, expired at the end of 2023 and has not been renewed. Check the Illinois State Board of Education for the current status of any state program before you budget.

Because public funding is limited, ask each microschool about payment plans, sibling discounts, and any private scholarships it offers. Some schools also accept third-party scholarship funds, so raise the question on your first call.

How to choose a microschool in Illinois

To choose a microschool in Illinois, match the school's model to your child, confirm the schedule and cost in writing, and visit during a normal school day. Start with the three schools above, then widen your search across the directory until you have two or three to tour.

Work through these steps:

  • Decide what your child needs most: gifted depth, homeschool support, or a learner-driven studio. Each school above answers a different need.
  • Confirm grades served, daily and weekly schedule, and full tuition in writing before you apply.
  • Visit during a regular school day and watch how students work, not just the tour script.
  • Ask how the school measures progress and how it communicates with parents.
  • Learn the vocabulary first. Terms like learner-driven and Socratic appear on most of these sites; our glossary of terms defines them.
