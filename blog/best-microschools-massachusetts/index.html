The best microschools in Massachusetts at a glance

The best microschools in Massachusetts depend on the model you want. Among the 22 microschools listed in our directory, three stand out for variety: Allium Montessori in Cambridge runs a classic elementary Montessori program for ages 6 to 12, North Star Teens in Sunderland offers self-directed learning for homeschooled teenagers, and Elements Academy in Braintree runs a project-based program for ages 3 to 18.

Massachusetts has 22 listed microschools, and most cluster in and around Cambridge. Our three picks below cover three different approaches, and each one runs a real, in-person program with a working website. See the full set on the hub page for microschools in Massachusetts.

Allium Montessori — Cambridge

Allium Montessori is a Montessori microschool in Cambridge serving students ages 6 to 12. Its integrated elementary Montessori curriculum carries tuition of $24,500 for the 2026–27 school year, with need-based financial assistance available through FAST.

Allium is part of the Wildflower network of small, teacher-led Montessori schools and sits on Massachusetts Avenue in Cambridge. Families apply on a rolling basis and can observe a class before applying. If you want a structured Montessori environment for an elementary-age child, this is the most established option among our Cambridge listings.

Cambridge carries 10 of the state's 22 listed microschools, and several are Montessori programs at a similar price point. What sets this school apart is the single-classroom, mixed-age elementary format that defines the Wildflower model, where one lead guide stays with a group of children for multiple years. Tour two or three Cambridge Montessori rooms back to back if you can, because the differences show up in the room, not the brochure.

North Star Teens — Sunderland

North Star Teens is a self-directed learning center in Sunderland that has operated since 1996. North Star is not a school in the traditional sense: it grants no diplomas, credits, or grades. Instead, North Star offers classes, one-on-one tutoring, adult mentoring, and a social community for homeschooled teenagers, who build an individual plan around their own interests.

Programming runs from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays, September through May. Families register their teen as a homeschooler with the local district and use North Star as the daytime base. This is the right fit for a self-motivated teenager who wants a community and a la carte classes rather than a fixed schedule.

Set in western Massachusetts, North Star serves the Pioneer Valley, so it is a regional option rather than a Boston-area one. The model has run for nearly three decades and has been studied as an example of self-directed education, which makes it unusually well documented for parents who want to read up before visiting. A teen who needs a tight daily structure and external accountability will likely do better in a more conventional microschool.

Elements Academy — Braintree

Elements Academy is a project-based microschool in Braintree serving ages 3 to 18. Its child-centered model is built on the idea that each student is seen and heard rather than moved through a fixed grade track.

That wide age range means Elements Academy works for families who want one program for siblings across early childhood through high school. Contact the school at its Brow Avenue location in Braintree to confirm current openings by age, since published tuition and class sizes are not listed online.

Braintree puts Elements Academy on the South Shore, roughly 20 minutes from Boston, which fills a gap for families who do not want to commute into Cambridge. Because the program spans ages 3 to 18 under one roof, ask specifically how it groups students and what a 14-year-old's day looks like versus a 5-year-old's. A single program covering that span works well for some families and feels too broad for others, so a tour matters more here than usual.

Our criteria for these Massachusetts microschools

This is an editor's pick, not a scored ranking. We chose three schools that are listed in our Massachusetts directory, have a working website we could verify, and represent different learning models. Montessori dominates the state count at 18 of 22 schools, so we deliberately included two non-Montessori programs to show the range a Massachusetts family actually has.

Each school's website was verified before we featured it, and we noted concrete program facts rather than marketing copy. We did not visit campuses or rate quality, so treat this as a starting shortlist. Browse every program on the hub for microschools in Massachusetts and tour the ones that fit before you decide.

How much do microschools cost in Massachusetts?

Microschool tuition in Massachusetts runs roughly $19,500 to $30,172 per year among the listed schools with published rates, with Montessori programs in Cambridge sitting at the higher end. Self-directed centers and part-time arrangements can cost less.

Massachusetts does not offer an education savings account or a private-school voucher, so families pay microschool tuition out of pocket. The state instead funds public options such as charter schools and inter-district school choice, both described on the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education site. Ask each school directly about sibling discounts and need-based aid, since published rates do not always reflect the final cost.

How to choose a microschool in Massachusetts

Start with the model. A Montessori room, a self-directed teen center, and a project-based program ask different things of a child, so match the model to how your child actually learns. Tour in person and watch a normal day rather than a staged open house.

Ask how many students are enrolled, what a typical day looks like, and how the school handles a child who is ahead in reading but behind in math. Confirm the legal setup: in Massachusetts, families enrolling in a part-time or non-diploma program usually register the child as a homeschooler with the local district.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Massachusetts?

Our directory lists 22 microschools in Massachusetts, spread across 11 cities. Cambridge alone accounts for 10 of them. The count grows as new programs open and get listed, so check the state hub for the current number.

How much do microschools cost in Massachusetts?

Among listed schools with published rates, microschool tuition in Massachusetts runs about $19,500 to $30,172 per year. Montessori programs in Cambridge sit at the high end. Self-directed centers and part-time schedules can cost noticeably less, and many schools offer need-based aid.

Does Massachusetts have an ESA or voucher for microschool tuition?

No. The state does not offer an education savings account or a private-school voucher, so there is no public money to put toward microschool tuition. Massachusetts funds public options such as charter schools and inter-district school choice instead. Families pay private microschool tuition out of pocket or through a school's own aid.

How do I choose a microschool in Massachusetts?

Start with the learning model and match it to your child, then tour in person on a normal school day. Ask about enrollment size, the daily schedule, and how the school supports a child who is uneven across subjects. Confirm the legal setup, since part-time and non-diploma programs in Massachusetts usually require registering the child as a homeschooler with the local district.

Are the microschools in this list legitimate?

These are real, listed schools, but a directory listing is not an endorsement. Verify licensing, safety, and current operating status yourself before enrolling. Each school here has a working website and an in-person program we checked, so still tour and confirm details directly.
