The best microschools in Kansas at a glance

Freedom Preparatory — Wichita

Freedom Preparatory is a Christian K-12 microschool in Wichita built on mastery-based learning. Freedom Preparatory places students by academic readiness rather than by age, so a student moves up when work is mastered instead of on a fixed grade calendar. Biblical instruction is integrated into every subject.

Two campuses run in Wichita: an elementary site on West 21st Street North and a secondary site on West Central Avenue. Depending on assessment results, a student may stay at one learning level for one, two, or three years, or skip a level.

Wichita holds 41 of the 46 microschools in our Kansas directory, so a family near Freedom Preparatory has more listed options to compare than anywhere else in the state. Families request a tour for current tuition and enrollment details.

Arise Microschool — Topeka

Arise Microschool is a personalized microschool in Topeka that uses a strengths-based approach. Arise Microschool keeps class sizes small and pairs technology with project-based learning, meeting each student at the student's current level and building an individual learning path from there. Family partnership and a small-group community sit at the center of the program.

This is the only listed microschool in Topeka in our Kansas directory, which makes Arise Microschool a distinct option for a capital-area family that wants a small, personalized setting outside Wichita.

Tuition is not published online, and the school asks families to reach out for cost and enrollment information. Check the grade range and the weekly schedule directly with Arise Microschool before applying.

Barefoot Technology Academy — Oberlin

Barefoot Technology Academy is a project-based, mastery-based virtual high school based in Oberlin. The academy serves gifted and neurodivergent learners through interest-driven projects in STEM, humanities, entrepreneurial, and creative work, with students building portfolios instead of sitting standard tests.

Rory Foulger founded the academy after spending seven years as Program Director of the Wolfram High School Summer Research Program. Instruction runs fully online with full-time and part-time enrollment, so the program reaches Kansas families well beyond Oberlin and the rural northwest corner of the state.

Because Barefoot Technology Academy is virtual, location matters less than fit. Families should check the project workload and the expected weekly hours before enrolling a student.

How much do microschools cost in Kansas?

Listed Kansas microschools do not publish a single tuition range in our directory, and the three schools here ask families to contact them for current pricing. Across the wider microschool market, tuition commonly runs from a few thousand dollars to the low teens per year, with part-time schedules costing less than full-time. Always confirm total cost, including fees, with the school directly.

Kansas does not run a broad education savings account program. Instead, the state funds private-school choice through the Tax Credit for Low Income Students Scholarship Program, administered by the Kansas State Department of Education. This program awards scholarships to eligible low-income students so they can attend an approved non-public school. Eligibility and the list of approved schools sit on the Kansas State Department of Education scholarship program page.

A microschool must be an approved scholarship-granting school for a family to use these funds there. Verify a school's status before you count on the scholarship, because a school that is not approved cannot accept it.
