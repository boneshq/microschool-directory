The best microschools in Colorado at a glance

Our three editors' picks for the best microschools in Colorado are Highlands Micro School in Denver, a K-5 student-centered program; Vail Valley Unbound in Eagle, an Acton-model learner-driven school; and The Farmstead School in Longmont, a Montessori middle-school collective. Highlands Micro School — Denver

Highlands Micro School is an independent K-5 microschool in Northwest Denver, at 3719 Perry Street. Classes stay small and run on curiosity and inquiry, with fieldwork, studio work, and an after-school option. A microschool this size lets teachers give each student personalized instruction.

Highlands Micro School centers learning on problem solving and social skills rather than a fixed lecture format. Highlands Micro School was featured in a 2023 Christian Science Monitor article on the rise of microschools, a sign that a microschool this small has drawn outside attention.

Families looking at Highlands Micro School in our directory should confirm current grade openings and pricing directly, since a microschool this size fills seats quickly.

Vail Valley Unbound — Eagle

Vail Valley Unbound is a learner-driven K-5 microschool in Eagle that follows the Acton Academy model. Students set their own pace, earn points for effort and badges for mastery, and work on real-world projects. Listed tuition is $16,060 per year, and the school is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

Students at Vail Valley Unbound pair technology-based work in reading, writing, and math with hands-on projects and outdoor immersion in the mountain valley. Vail Valley Unbound is accredited by the International Association of Learning Design Schools and runs summer programs, including a Children's Business Fair.

See the full listing for Vail Valley Unbound to schedule a tour. An Acton-model microschool runs differently from a Montessori room, so a visit matters before enrolling.

The Farmstead School — Longmont

The Farmstead School is a Montessori collective near Niwot, at Oxford Road in Longmont, serving middle-school students. A student-directed, hands-on curriculum connects subjects across disciplines, and the program pairs academics with farm work, arts, and device-free learning. The Farmstead School is a partner of the KaiPod Learning microschool network.

This Montessori collective runs as a homeschool group rather than a conventional classroom building. Unstructured play and an intentional, low-tech culture set this Montessori microschool apart from screen-heavy programs.

Read the The Farmstead School listing for contact details. A middle-school Montessori collective is a narrow niche, so families should confirm the grade range and weekly schedule before applying.

How much do microschools cost in Colorado?

Listed microschool tuition in Colorado runs from $1,440 to $16,060 per year, based on the schools in our directory. Price depends on the model, the city, and the number of days per week. Some schools post a free or sliding-scale option for certain grades, so the low end of that range is real, not a teaser.

Colorado has no statewide private-school ESA or voucher, so most families pay microschool tuition directly. Families who teach at home instead file a notice of intent with a school district under state homeschool law; the Colorado Department of Education homeschool office publishes the current rules. Ask each school about its own scholarships or aid.

How to choose a microschool in Colorado

To choose a microschool in Colorado, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or Acton learner-driven, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just price. If a term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common microschool and homeschool models.
