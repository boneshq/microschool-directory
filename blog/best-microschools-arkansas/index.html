ACRES Microschool and Homeschool Hybrid — Brookland

ACRES Microschool and Homeschool Hybrid serves students from kindergarten through seventh grade in the Brookland and Jonesboro area of northeast Arkansas. The program runs on self-paced, mastery-based instruction in reading and math, paired with project-based work. A hybrid format lets families combine microschool days with home learning.

Reading and math at ACRES move at each student's own pace, so a child advances once a skill is mastered rather than on a fixed calendar. Project work then gives students room to follow personal interests and build on their strengths. The school was enrolling for the 2026-27 year when we checked its site.

Families looking at ACRES Microschool and Homeschool Hybrid in our directory should confirm current grade openings, the weekly schedule, and pricing directly with the school.

The Learning Connection — DeWitt

The Learning Connection is a project-based microschool in DeWitt, in the Arkansas Delta, serving students in grades K through 5. The school works alongside families to support a homeschool journey, setting personalized academic and personal goals for each child. Classes are tailored to individual needs so learners can progress at their own pace.

Hands-on, real-world projects anchor the curriculum here, connecting academic concepts to practical life. Rather than run as a conventional classroom building, the program is built to partner with homeschooling parents on a shared plan for each student.

Read the listing for The Learning Connection to find contact details. A homeschool-partner microschool runs differently from a full-day private school, so ask how the weekly schedule and parent role work before enrolling.

Hollyhock Montessori — Fort Smith

Hollyhock Montessori is an authentic Montessori program at 616 S. 17th Street in Fort Smith, serving children ages 3 to 6. The school is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit and was entering its third year when we checked. Children learn through self-directed work in a small, mixed-age community of peers.

Hollyhock is a member of the Wildflower Network, a national group of small, teacher-led Montessori schools, and describes itself as the first Wildflower school in Arkansas. Seats are limited; the school listed only a handful of openings for the 2026-27 year at the time of our review.

See the full listing for Hollyhock Montessori to schedule a visit. Because this program serves only the early years, confirm the age range and daily schedule before applying.

How much do microschools cost in Arkansas?

None of the Arkansas microschools in our directory publish tuition online, so we cannot list a price range for the state. Cost generally depends on the model, the city, and the number of days per week. Contact each school directly for current pricing, and ask whether it offers its own sliding-scale aid or scholarships.

Arkansas does fund private and home education through the Children's Educational Freedom Account program, created by the 2023 LEARNS Act and run by the Arkansas Department of Education. The program reached universal eligibility for K-12 students in 2025-26 and can be used for private-school tuition and fees, so many families ask whether a given microschool qualifies. Families who teach at home instead file a notice of intent with their school district under state homeschool law; the Arkansas Department of Education home school office publishes the current rules and deadlines.

How to choose a microschool in Arkansas

To choose a microschool in Arkansas, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or project-based, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just convenience. When a term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common microschool and homeschool models.
