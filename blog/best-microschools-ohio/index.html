The best microschools in Ohio at a glance

Three Ohio microschools stand out for distinct models and locations: Bloom Learning Community in Westlake (a learner-centered hybrid for ages 5 to 10), Kingdom Scholars Microschool in Xenia (a faith-based K-5 program), and Montessori EARTH School in Columbus (a project-based program for grades 8 to 12). Bloom Learning Community — Westlake

Bloom Learning Community is a learner-centered microschool in Westlake, Ohio, serving children ages 5 to 10. Bloom Learning Community runs a hybrid schedule built for homeschool families, founded in 2019 and operating as a 501(c)(3) non-profit at 23855 Detroit Road. Daily work emphasizes self-direction and creative process over a fixed grade-level track.

A partnership with Baldwin Wallace University gives Bloom students access to a college campus and its resources. Enrollment stays small at Bloom Learning Community, so each child works at an individual pace rather than moving with a single class.

Families inquire by scheduling a tour. Browse the Bloom Learning Community listing for contact details and current enrollment status.

Kingdom Scholars Microschool — Xenia

Kingdom Scholars Microschool is a faith-based K-5 microschool in Xenia, Ohio, at 436 E. Main Street. Kingdom Scholars Microschool combines a standard academic curriculum with biblical worldview instruction and character work, and keeps class sizes small so teachers can give individual attention. The program is taking applications for the 2026 school year.

Learning at Kingdom Scholars Microschool follows personalized academic trajectories rather than a single whole-class pace. Parents who want a Christ-centered elementary setting with a small student body are the clear fit here.

Tours are available, and tuition details come on request. Open the Kingdom Scholars Microschool listing to start that conversation.

Montessori EARTH School — Columbus

Montessori EARTH School is a project-based microschool in Columbus, Ohio, serving grades 8 to 12 at 2344 Woodward Avenue. Learning here is built around real projects, interdisciplinary work, and experiential outdoor time, with limited enrollment and a relationship-based setting. This program fills a gap most Ohio microschools leave open by serving middle and high school students.

Where many Ohio microschools stop at elementary grades, this program runs through high school. That breadth makes Montessori EARTH School worth a look for families who want a small, project-driven setting for an older student.

Parts of the school's website are being rebuilt, so confirm the current schedule and tuition directly. Open the Montessori EARTH School listing for contact information.

Why these three Ohio microschools

This is an editor's pick, not a scored ranking. We chose three schools that are listed in our Ohio directory, have a working website we could verify, and represent different learning models and cities. We did not rate one school as better than another, and we did not accept payment for placement.

Our criteria were simple. First, each school had to appear in our directory of microschools in Ohio. Second, each school's website had to load and describe an operating program, so we skipped listings with dead or flagged sites. Third, we wanted variety: a learner-centered program, a faith-based program, and a secondary-level program, in three separate cities.

Plenty of other Ohio microschools could fit a given family better. Use the directory to compare every listing, then verify details with the school before you enroll.

How much do microschools cost in Ohio?

Most Ohio microschools in our directory do not publish tuition, and the lowest published figure we have on record is around $979 per month. Several Ohio Montessori programs run tuition-free for kindergarten through second grade when families use public funding. Because pricing varies widely by program, ask each school directly for current tuition and any fees.

Ohio families can offset tuition through the state's EdChoice Scholarship, which provides vouchers toward private and qualifying private-school tuition. Ohio also runs the Ohio ACE educational savings program for enrichment expenses. Check eligibility and award amounts on the Ohio Department of Education scholarships page before you assume a school is out of reach.

A program that looks expensive at list price can become affordable once a scholarship or savings account is applied, so price each school after funding, not before.

Choosing a microschool in Ohio: a checklist

To choose a microschool in Ohio, start with the directory, shortlist schools by model and location, then tour your top two or three. Confirm grades served, schedule, class size, tuition after any scholarship, and the teacher's background. Ask how the school measures progress and how it handles a student who falls behind.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Ohio?

Eighteen microschools in Ohio are listed in our directory across 13 cities. Cincinnati, Cleveland, and Columbus hold the most listings. The real number statewide is higher, since new microschools open faster than any directory can verify them.

How much do microschools cost in Ohio?

Most Ohio microschools do not publish tuition. We have one published figure on record as low as about $979 per month, and several Montessori programs run tuition-free for kindergarten through second grade using public funding. Ask each school for current tuition and fees.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Ohio?

The state runs the EdChoice Scholarship, which provides vouchers toward qualifying private-school tuition, and the Ohio ACE program, an educational savings account for enrichment costs. Confirm eligibility and award amounts on the Ohio Department of Education scholarships page, then ask whether your chosen school accepts the funding.

What should I look for when choosing a microschool in Ohio?

Start with our Ohio directory, shortlist by learning model and city, then tour your top two or three. Confirm grades served, schedule, class size, and tuition after any scholarship. Ask how each school measures progress and supports a student who falls behind.

Are microschools in Ohio legit?

Yes, microschools are a real and growing option in Ohio, ranging from faith-based K-5 programs to project-based secondary schools. They are not all regulated the same way, so verify a school's registration or licensing status and tour the program before you enroll.
