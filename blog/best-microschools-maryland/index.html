Indigo Montessori — Baltimore

Indigo Montessori is a nonprofit Montessori microschool in Baltimore, at 3451 Falls Rd in the Hampden area, serving children ages 5 to 9. The school is a registered 501(c)(3) and a member of the Wildflower Network, an ecosystem of small, decentralized Montessori microschools. A program this size lets a guide give each child individualized instruction.

Indigo describes its room as child-centered, built on freedom and responsibility rather than coercion. Families pick the school for individualized work, plenty of outdoor time, and limited screen use. Tuition runs on an income-based sliding scale set between 0 and 14 percent of a family's income, so the cost tracks what a household can pay.

Families looking at Indigo Montessori in our directory should confirm current openings and where they land on the sliding scale, since a Montessori room this small fills its mixed-age group carefully.

The Aspiring Phoenix Foundation — Joppatowne

The Aspiring Phoenix Foundation is a learner-driven microschool in Joppatowne, at 701 Whitaker Mill Road, that runs on the Acton Academy network model. Students follow a self-directed approach that blends Montessori, Reggio Emilia, and Waldorf influences with modern tools. Listed tuition is $585 per month.

The school runs an 11-month calendar of short learning sessions with breaks between them, rather than a standard September-to-June year. Its focus is curiosity, independence, and child-directed work, which sets an Acton-style room apart from a teacher-led classroom.

See the full listing for The Aspiring Phoenix Foundation to schedule a visit. An Acton-model microschool runs differently from a conventional school, so a tour matters before enrolling, and you should confirm the grade range directly.

The SOAR Academy — Randallstown

The SOAR Academy is a project-based microschool in Randallstown serving students from kindergarten through fifth grade. Founded by Andréa Harper, M.Ed., the school runs an intentional eight-block daily schedule that folds math, reading, writing, science, and social studies together with entrepreneurship, financial literacy, STEM, leadership, and physical wellness.

SOAR keeps classes small and centers personalized support inside that full academic program. The posted tuition covers school supplies, breakfast, lunch, daily snacks, and access to summer programming, including an Entrepreneur and Fitness Summer Program.

Read the The SOAR Academy listing for contact details. Because the school does not publish a flat tuition figure, ask what the all-inclusive rate works out to before you apply.

How much do microschools cost in Maryland?

Maryland microschools publish little pricing, so plan to ask each school directly. The two figures we could confirm are The Aspiring Phoenix Foundation at $585 per month and Indigo Montessori on an income-based sliding scale between 0 and 14 percent of family income. The other schools in our directory list no flat tuition, which is common for programs this small.

Maryland has no education savings account for private tuition. Its one private school-choice program is the BOOST voucher, which helps students eligible for free or reduced-price meals attend a participating nonpublic school; a microschool has to enroll in BOOST for families to use it, and not all do. Families who teach at home instead file a notification form with their local school system under home instruction law, and the Maryland State Department of Education home instruction office publishes the current rules. Ask each school about its own scholarships or aid.

How to choose a microschool in Maryland

To choose a microschool in Maryland, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or Acton learner-driven, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just price. If a term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common microschool and homeschool models.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Maryland?

How much does a microschool cost in Maryland?

Does Maryland have an ESA or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Its one private school-choice program is the BOOST voucher, which helps students eligible for free or reduced-price meals attend a participating nonpublic school. A microschool must enroll in BOOST for families to use it, and not all do. Most microschool families pay tuition directly or use a school's own aid.

How do I choose a microschool in Maryland?

Start with the learning model that fits your child, such as Montessori or Acton learner-driven. Confirm the grades served, the weekly schedule, and the class size. Tour the school, ask how teachers track progress, and read the enrollment terms. Our parent guide walks through the full checklist.

Are microschools in Maryland legit?

Yes. A Maryland microschool is a small private school or a homeschool collective. Families operating under home instruction law file a notification form with their local school system 15 days before the year starts. Each program we feature has a working website and verified program details, but parents should still confirm licensing and references directly.
