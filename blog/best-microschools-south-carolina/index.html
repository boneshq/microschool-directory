A Home for School — Moncks Corner

A Home for School is a microschool in Moncks Corner serving students from kindergarten through eighth grade. The program runs on a self-paced, student-centered model, so each learner moves at their own rhythm rather than on a fixed grade-level schedule. A microschool this size lets teachers tailor instruction to one child at a time.

The school describes a loving, diversity-minded environment and folds in outdoor learning and hands-on STEM experiments alongside core academics. Its stated aim is to honor the culture each family brings, which shapes how the day is built around the student.

Families looking at A Home for School in our directory should contact the school directly to confirm current grade openings, the weekly schedule, and pricing, since the site does not publish those details.

Trinity Homeschool Academy — Columbia

Trinity Homeschool Academy is a project-based program in the Columbia area that runs hybrid learning pods for grades K-5, with additional classes for middle and high school students. The school describes its mission as equipping the next generation of homeschooling families, and it pairs small group instruction with parent-led learning at home.

One feature the academy highlights is that its teachers homeschool their own children while instructing others, which it presents as a shared stake in student success. Families partner with the program and stay accountable through their own homeschool association, a common arrangement under South Carolina law.

See the full listing for Trinity Homeschool Academy to reach the director and ask about pod days, class sizes, and grade ranges. A hybrid pod runs differently from a full-week classroom, so confirm the in-person schedule before enrolling.

Acton Academy Greenville — Greenville

Acton Academy Greenville is a learner-driven microschool in Greenville that follows the Acton Academy model. Students set personal goals, work in mixed-age studios, and take on real-world projects rather than sitting through lectures. The school is part of a global network of more than 300 Acton Academies.

A typical day pairs focused work time driven by each student's own goals with collaboration, discussion, and time to reflect and revise. The school invites prospective families to read the Acton book Courage to Grow first and offers a trial "audition" week so a child can experience the studio before committing.

Read the Acton Academy Greenville listing to schedule that trial. An Acton-model microschool works very differently from a traditional classroom, so a visit matters before enrolling.

How much do microschools cost in South Carolina?

None of the South Carolina schools in our directory publish a tuition figure, so we do not list a state price range. Cost depends on the model, the city, and the number of days per week, and several of these programs run only part of the week. Contact each school directly for current pricing and any sliding-scale or scholarship options.

South Carolina does run a state scholarship account, the Education Scholarship Trust Fund, for eligible K-12 students. A 2024 state Supreme Court ruling barred using those funds for private-school tuition, but 2025 legislation restored that use; the award is $7,634 per student for the 2026-27 school year, up from $7,500 in 2025-26, according to the state ESTF FAQ; income limits apply, and the program is capped, so confirm current rules with the South Carolina Department of Education. Families who teach at home instead often join a homeschool accountability association under the state's Option 3 law; the South Carolina Department of Education home schooling office lists the current options.

How to choose a microschool in South Carolina

To choose a microschool in South Carolina, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as project-based or Acton learner-driven, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Because several of these programs meet only part of the week, check how the at-home days are structured. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just price.
