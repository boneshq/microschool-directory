The best microschools in Texas at a glance

Three Texas microschools stand out across different cities and models: Acton Academy Austin Main Campus, a learner-driven program in Austin for grades 1-12; Mirus Academy, a small college-prep microschool in Katy serving grades 8-12; and ESTEAM Academy, a learner-driven STEAM program in Round Rock for ages 4 and up. Each is listed in our directory of 102 Texas microschools and runs an active website.

Acton Academy Austin Main Campus — Austin

Acton Academy Austin Main Campus is a learner-driven microschool in Austin that launched in 2009 and serves grades 1-12. Students work across three studios — Elementary, Middle School, and Launchpad — at their own pace, earning points for effort and badges for mastery rather than sitting through traditional lectures. Yearly tuition runs $14,894.

Daily work is built around a "Hero's Journey" framework, with students setting their own goals and collaborating on real-world projects. Core academic skills are practiced through technology-enhanced, self-paced work instead of whole-class instruction.

Austin is the densest microschool market in Texas, with 14 listed schools, so a parent comparing options here has room to tour several. Acton Academy Austin Main Campus is one of the longest-running learner-driven programs in that group.

Mirus Academy — Katy

Mirus Academy is a small college-prep microschool in Katy that has operated since 2006 and serves grades 8-12. This Mirus Academy program describes its approach as college-preparatory academics in a small, lower-stress environment, with personalized attention that a larger school cannot match. Accreditation comes from Cognia and the National Independent Private School Association (NIPSA).

Running entirely on tuition, Mirus Academy takes no federal or state funding, which keeps the program independent of public-school mandates. Its campus sits at 5506 3rd Street in Katy, west of Houston.

For families whose child is heading into middle or high school and wants a structured, academic path rather than a fully self-directed one, Mirus Academy is a different shape of microschool from the learner-driven studios elsewhere on this list.

ESTEAM Academy — Round Rock

ESTEAM Academy is a learner-driven STEAM microschool in Round Rock, affiliated with Acton Academy, that serves ages 4 and up across four studios. Experiential learning with Socratic guidance drives the day, and ESTEAM Academy publishes its tuition openly: $1,200 per month for a 5-day week, scaling down to $850 per month for a 2-day option.

A flexible 2-to-5-day schedule is the practical draw here. Families that want to keep some days at home, or test a microschool before committing full time, can do that without changing schools.

Like other Acton-affiliated programs, ESTEAM Academy frames its work around character development and independent learning rather than lectures and tests, which keeps it consistent with the learner-driven model used across Texas.

How much do microschools cost in Texas?

Across the 102 Texas microschools in our directory, published tuition ranges from about $1,200 per year on the low end to roughly $15,500 per year on the high end, with most programs landing somewhere in between. Many schools, including ESTEAM Academy, price by the number of days per week, so a part-time schedule costs less than a full five-day week.

Texas now has a school-choice program that can offset some of that cost. Known as the Texas Education Freedom Accounts (TEFA) program and administered by the Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts, this program lets approved families use state funds for private-school tuition and other approved education expenses. Read the program details and eligibility on the official Texas Education Freedom Accounts program site before you assume a given microschool qualifies.

Always confirm current tuition with the school directly. Published numbers change between school years, and many microschools list a separate application or enrollment fee on top of tuition.

How to choose a microschool in Texas

To choose a microschool in Texas, match the learning model to your child, confirm grades and schedule, verify accreditation or funding eligibility, and tour in person before enrolling. Start by deciding whether your child needs a structured college-prep path or a self-directed studio, then shortlist schools in your city that fit.

Practical steps:

  • Learn the model names first. A learner-driven studio, a college-prep microschool, and a Montessori program run very differently — our glossary of terms defines each one.
  • Check grades and schedule against your child's age. Some Texas microschools serve grades 1-12; others, like Mirus Academy, start at grade 8.
  • Ask about accreditation and funding. Confirm whether a school is accredited and whether it accepts Texas Education Freedom Accounts funds.
  • Tour in person and ask to see a normal day, not a staged one.

If you are new to this model, our complete guide to microschools walks through how a microschool differs from homeschooling and traditional private school, and what questions to ask on a tour.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Texas?

Our directory lists 102 microschools across Texas, spread over 54 cities. Austin has the most at 14, followed by Houston with 8 and San Antonio with 7. The mix runs from learner-driven and project-based programs to college-prep and Montessori models.

How much do microschools cost in Texas?

Published tuition for Texas microschools in our directory runs from about $1,200 to roughly $15,500 per year. Many schools price by the number of days per week, so a 2-day or 3-day schedule costs less than full-time. Always confirm the current figure, plus any application or enrollment fee, with the school directly.

Can I use a Texas ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Possibly. The state now runs the Texas Education Freedom Accounts (TEFA) program through the Comptroller, which lets approved families use state funds for private-school tuition and other approved expenses. Whether a specific microschool qualifies depends on the program rules and the school's status, so check both before enrolling.

How do I choose a microschool in Texas?

Match the learning model to your child first, then confirm grades, schedule, and accreditation, and tour in person before enrolling. Decide whether your child needs a structured college-prep path or a self-directed studio, shortlist schools in your city, and ask each one to walk you through a normal day.

Are microschools in Texas legit?

Many are established, accredited programs. Mirus Academy in Katy, for example, has run since 2006 and is accredited by Cognia and NIPSA. Texas does not accredit microschools centrally, so verify each school's accreditation, history, and enrollment process yourself rather than assuming.
