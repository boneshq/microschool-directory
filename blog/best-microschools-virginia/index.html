The best microschools in Virginia at a glance

Three Virginia microschools stand out for distinct, verified programs: Solstice Hybrid Academy in Norfolk runs a mobile STEAM program for ages 5–10, Heartwise Scholars Microschool in Chesapeake serves K–6 on a four-day schedule, and Vida Academy in Henrico teaches ages 4–9 in a bilingual English-Spanish model. Solstice Hybrid Academy — Norfolk

Solstice Hybrid Academy is a mobile, community-based microschool in Norfolk for learners ages 5–10. Each day of the week covers a different element of STEAM, and instruction starts on a converted school bus before students travel to enrichment sites around the city. Solstice operates from 4909 Colley Avenue in Norfolk.

This format suits families who want their child learning in real settings rather than a single classroom. In practice, each day pairs a STEAM focus with a destination tied to that subject, so science, technology, engineering, art, and math get dedicated time.

Parents in the Norfolk area looking for an active, experiential schedule and a small group are the clear fit here. Contact the school directly for current tuition, age cutoffs, and seat availability.

Heartwise Scholars Microschool — Chesapeake

Heartwise Scholars Microschool is a K–6 microschool in Chesapeake that opened in August 2024 and grew from 17 students in its first year to 23 in its second. The school runs a four-day model called "Slay and Play," with morning academics and enrichment on Mondays and Wednesdays and study halls, park time, and passion projects on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

A mixed-age, one-room-schoolhouse structure lets Heartwise Scholars Microschool track each child's academic progress individually. Social-emotional learning and relationships sit alongside core academics, which works for families who want a small, personalized setting.

This Chesapeake microschool operates at 1400 Kempsville Road, Suite 105. Families should confirm the current grade range, the four-day week, and tuition before they apply.

Vida Academy — Henrico

Vida Academy is a bilingual microschool in Henrico for ages 4–9, part of the KaiPod Learning network. Students receive daily instruction in both English and Spanish, delivered through project-based, collaborative work in small groups at 8001 Strath Road in Henrico.

A bilingual model is the clearest reason a family would choose Vida Academy over a single-language program. Daily Spanish instruction builds fluency over time rather than treating it as a separate subject, and small class sizes let the pace match each student.

Enrollment for the 2026–2027 school year is open. Contact the school for the daily schedule, the full age range, and tuition, since those details are set at the school level.

Why these three Virginia microschools made our list

These three schools are an editor's pick, not a scored ranking. All three are listed in our Virginia directory, all three have a working website we checked, and together they show a range of models and cities — a mobile STEAM program in Norfolk, a four-day mixed-age microschool in Chesapeake, and a bilingual program in Henrico.

We deliberately varied the learning model and the metro area instead of picking three schools from the same brand or the same city. Every school here is operating now and serving students, and we skipped listings whose sites were dead or that turned out not to be microschools.

None of this is a quality score. A school that fits one family may not fit another, so treat the list as a place to begin. Your next step is to read the full state directory and the program details on each school's site.

How much do microschools cost in Virginia?

Among Virginia microschools that publish a figure in our directory, tuition reaches roughly $9,600 per year, though many schools do not list a public price and several charge less. Microschools are usually cheaper than traditional private schools because they run with small staffs and shared space, but the range is wide and you should confirm tuition with each school.

Virginia does not run a statewide education savings account (ESA) for K–12 private tuition. The state's main private-school funding route is the Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits Program, which gives tax credits to donors who fund scholarship foundations that award private-school tuition scholarships to eligible students. Income limits apply, and families apply through an approved scholarship foundation rather than the state directly.

For the current rules and the list of approved scholarship foundations, start at the Virginia Department of Education, then ask each microschool whether it accepts scholarship students.

How to choose a microschool in Virginia

To choose a Virginia microschool, match the learning model to your child, verify the basics in person, and confirm the money before you commit. Visit during a school day, count how many students attend, check the staff-to-student ratio, and request the daily schedule and the full tuition figure in writing.

Work through these steps:

  • Decide which model fits: project-based, classical, Montessori, bilingual, or a general microschool. Our glossary of terms explains what each model means.
  • Tour in person and watch a real day, not a staged one, then find out how the school handles different ages and reading levels in one room.
  • Confirm cost in writing — tuition, fees, and whether the school accepts Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits Program scholarships.
  • Check the calendar: the number of days per week, the hours, and what families handle at home on off days.
  • Find out how the school measures progress and how often it reports to parents.
