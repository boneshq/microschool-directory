Ivy Greene: An Acton Academy — Pontotoc

Ivy Greene is a learner-driven microschool in Pontotoc that follows the Acton Academy model. Students set their own pace through self-paced work, Socratic discussion, and experiential projects, and the school runs without standardized tests, homework, or busywork. Listed tuition is $655 a month over an 11-month year, which works out to $7,200 annually.

The program describes its aim as learning to learn, learning to do, and learning to be, with guides leading discussion rather than lecturing. Ivy Greene frames the goal as raising curious, independent learners who can pursue a calling of their own.

Families looking at Ivy Greene: An Acton Academy in our directory should confirm current grade openings and the daily schedule directly. An Acton-model microschool runs differently from a conventional classroom, so a visit matters before enrolling.

Harper Learning Academy — Byram

Harper Learning Academy is a nonprofit microschool at 7120 Siwell Road in Byram, in Hinds County, serving students from the elementary grades through high school. The school is built for students with learning differences and pairs small classes with hands-on, project-based work and competency-based grading. Founder Stephanie Harper, Ph.D., is a nationally board-certified educator with 26 years in the field.

Instruction draws on several learning modalities, from visual and kinesthetic to interpersonal, so teachers can match the approach to each student. Research-based strategies and student-centered pedagogy anchor the day rather than a single fixed format.

See the listing for Harper Learning Academy to ask about tuition and current openings. A microschool focused on learning differences keeps enrollment small, so confirm the grade range and seats before applying.

Empowered Prep Academy — Jackson

Empowered Prep Academy is a project-based microschool at 1831 Robinson Street in Jackson, serving grades 3 through 12. The school specializes in students who have or show characteristics of dyslexia and other learning differences, and it offers both part-time and full-time plans. Empowered Prep is a partner of the KaiPod Learning microschool network.

The program blends a personalized, student-centered approach with the small, collaborative feel of a microschool. Staff and specialists support diverse learning needs, and the curriculum works to raise family awareness of dyslexia alongside core academics.

Read the Empowered Prep Academy listing for contact details and a tour. Because plans range from part-time to full-time, ask how the weekly schedule and pricing fit your child before you enroll.

How much do microschools cost in Mississippi?

Only one Mississippi microschool in our directory publishes tuition: Ivy Greene, at $655 a month over an 11-month year, or $7,200 a year. Most schools in the state do not publish a number and ask families to contact them, so plan to call or email for current pricing. Cost depends on the model, the city, and whether a plan is part-time or full-time.

Mississippi has no universal ESA. Its only education savings account is the Equal Opportunity for Students with Special Needs Program, which is limited to students who have had an active IEP within the past three years and is capped by an annual appropriation, so eligible families can be waitlisted. Families who teach at home instead file a certificate of enrollment with the local school attendance officer by August 15 each year; the Mississippi Department of Education home school office publishes the current rules. Ask each school about its own scholarships or aid.

How to choose a microschool in Mississippi

To choose a microschool in Mississippi, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Acton learner-driven or project-based, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. If your child has an IEP or a diagnosed learning difference, ask how the school supports it, since several Mississippi programs are built around that need. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just price. If a term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common microschool and homeschool models.

Does Mississippi have an ESA or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Mississippi has no universal ESA. Its only education savings account is the Equal Opportunity for Students with Special Needs Program, which is limited to students who have had an active IEP within the past three years. Funding is capped, so eligible families can be waitlisted. Most other families pay microschool tuition directly or use a school's own aid.
