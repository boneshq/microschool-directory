The School of Harvest — Washington, DC

The School of Harvest is an independent Montessori school for early adolescents at 5200 Foote St NE, east of the river, serving grades 6 through 8. It is a member of the Wildflower network of small, decentralized Montessori schools, and it pairs one intentionally small indoor classroom with outdoor learning on a farm it uses through a partnership with Washington Parks and People.

The day follows the Montessori adolescent model: a morning Growth Circle, then two work cycles of about three hours each that move between academic lessons, hands-on projects, and independent study. Students rotate through roles on the farm, in the kitchen, and in small production-and-exchange projects, and monthly outings to farms, museums, and local businesses carry the learning across the city.

Families looking at The School of Harvest in our directory should ask about openings for the current year, since its admissions page still describes the 2025-26 enrollment cycle. Tuition is tiered and need-based rather than one published rate, so ask the school which tier fits your family.

ANANSI Academy — Washington, DC

ANANSI Academy is a learner-driven microschool in Washington, DC serving high-school grades 9-12. The program runs on project-based, self-directed learning and partners with Changemaker Education. Students take ownership of their work rather than moving through a fixed lecture schedule.

A learner-driven high school puts more weight on student-chosen projects and pacing than a traditional classroom does. Because the school keeps a light public web presence, families should treat the listing as a starting point and confirm details by reaching out.

See the full listing for ANANSI Academy to request current information. A self-directed program runs differently from a Montessori room, so a conversation and a visit matter before enrolling.

The Riverseed School — Washington, DC

The Riverseed School is a Montessori microschool at 913 55th St NE, in the Burrville neighborhood east of the river. It is planned as a PK3-5th school: it enrolls PK3 through 3rd grade in 2026-27 and plans to serve grades through 5th by 2028-29. It runs as a two-classroom schoolhouse with mixed-age, child-directed rooms, and as a campus of DC Wildflower Public Charter School, part of the Wildflower network, it is tuition-free and admits students through the My School DC lottery.

As a Wildflower school, Riverseed follows that network's principles, including authentic Montessori practice, teacher-led governance, a nature focus, and ties to the surrounding community. Outdoor learning and environmental stewardship sit at the center of the day rather than at the edges.

Read the The Riverseed School listing for contact details, and apply through My School DC. An early-grades Montessori room is a narrow band, so families should confirm the current grade range and their waitlist position before counting on a seat.

How much do microschools cost in the District of Columbia?

The Riverseed School and Blue Montessori are campuses of DC Wildflower Public Charter School, so they charge no tuition and fill seats through the My School DC lottery. None of the private DC microschools in our directory publish a flat tuition online, and The School of Harvest uses tiered, need-based rates, so there is no public price range to quote. Contact each private school directly for current tuition, aid, and openings.

Washington, DC runs the federally funded DC Opportunity Scholarship Program, an income-based voucher that covers private-school tuition and related fees for eligible students; applications run through Serving Our Children. Families who teach at home instead file a Notification of Intent to Homeschool with OSSE; the Office of the State Superintendent of Education homeschooling office publishes the current rules. Ask each school whether it accepts the scholarship and about its own aid.

How to choose a microschool in the District of Columbia

To choose a microschool in Washington, DC, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or learner-driven, to how your child learns best.

Find out how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, not just price. If a term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common microschool and homeschool models.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Washington, DC?

As of September 2026, microschool.directory lists 8 microschools in Washington, DC. All sit within the District itself, so families compare them by neighborhood and model rather than by city. The set covers Montessori, general microschool, and learner-driven programs. New programs open often, so the count grows through the year.

How much does a microschool cost in Washington, DC?

The Riverseed School and Blue Montessori are campuses of DC Wildflower Public Charter School, so they charge no tuition and fill seats through the My School DC lottery. None of the private DC microschools in our directory publish a flat tuition online, and The School of Harvest uses tiered, need-based rates, so contact each private school directly for current tuition, aid, and openings. Income-eligible families may also qualify for the DC Opportunity Scholarship Program.

Does Washington, DC have a voucher or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Yes. The DC Opportunity Scholarship Program is a federally funded, income-based voucher that covers private-school tuition and related fees for eligible DC students. Applications run through Serving Our Children, and award amounts adjust each year. Confirm with each school that it accepts the scholarship before you apply.

How do I choose a microschool in Washington, DC?

Start with the learning model that fits your child, such as Montessori or learner-driven. Confirm the grades served, the weekly schedule, and the class size. Tour the school, ask how teachers track progress, and read the enrollment terms. Our parent guide walks through the full checklist.

Are microschools in Washington, DC legit?

Yes. A DC microschool is usually a small private school or a homeschool collective, though DC Wildflower runs its Montessori microschools as public charter schools. Families teaching at home file a Notification of Intent to Homeschool with OSSE. Each program we feature has a working website or a verified directory listing, but parents should still confirm licensing and references directly.
