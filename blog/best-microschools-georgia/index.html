Acton Academy Blairsville — Blairsville

Acton Academy Blairsville is a learner-driven microschool in Blairsville, in north Georgia's mountains. This school runs three days a week of scheduled in-person learning, with the remaining days left open for a student's own projects. Applications for the 2026/27 school year are open.

Its model builds the week around three goals the school states plainly: learning to learn, learning to do, and learning to be. Practical skills come through hands-on Quests, and character development runs alongside the academics.

Outdoor learning and collaborative work anchor the program, which fits a rural setting better than a fixed-bell schedule. Acton Academy Blairsville publishes only a $35 application fee, so ask the school directly for current tuition and the part-time options.

The Attuned Community School — Centerville

The Attuned Community School is a project- and nature-based microschool in Centerville, near Warner Robins in middle Georgia. Students at The Attuned Community School spend 90 or more minutes outdoors every day through gardening, hiking, and nature activities. Play here is treated as a real channel for learning, not a break from it.

Days at The Attuned Community School run on play-based and inquiry-based instruction, with a stated focus on emotional processing, self-regulation, and setting boundaries. That structure suits families who want a slower, outdoor-heavy environment for younger children.

No tuition figure is published, so contact The Attuned Community School for current pricing, grade ranges, and the daily schedule before you apply.

The Ferguson School — Decatur

The Ferguson School is a Christian microschool in Decatur, just east of Atlanta, that teaches a STREAM curriculum, science, technology, reading, engineering, arts, and math. Its lower school covers kindergarten through 6th grade, and The Ferguson School offers both a private-school track and a hybrid homeschool track.

Ferguson describes itself as progressive and culturally inclusive, with the faith component woven through the academics rather than bolted on. Located at 308 Clairmont Avenue in Decatur, the school sits inside the metro Atlanta area that holds the largest cluster of Georgia microschools.

Because The Ferguson School offers both full private enrollment and a part-time hybrid option, ask which track has open seats for your child's grade, and confirm current tuition, when you reach out.

How much do microschools cost in Georgia?

Microschool tuition in Georgia ranges from about $35 to roughly $12,000 a year across the schools in our directory, based on published figures. Most Georgia microschools do not post tuition online, so the real cost for a given school usually requires a direct inquiry. Part-time and hybrid tracks generally cost less than full-time enrollment.

Such a wide range reflects how different these schools are. A small co-op or a two-day hybrid program sits at the low end, while a full-time, full-week microschool with a building and paid guides sits near the top.

Georgia families can offset tuition through the Georgia Promise Scholarship, the state's first education savings account program, passed in 2024. That program provides eligible students up to $6,500 per year for private-school tuition, tutoring, and other approved expenses, with eligibility tied to public-school enrollment history and attendance zone. Confirm current rules and application windows on the state's official page at the Georgia Student Finance Commission.

Choosing a microschool in Georgia: a parent's checklist

To choose a microschool in Georgia, start with the learning model, learner-driven, project-based, Montessori, or faith-based, then check the grade range, the weekly schedule, the cost, and whether the program accepts the Georgia Promise Scholarship. Visit in person and ask how a typical day runs before you commit.

Practical steps:

  • Match the model to your child. A learner-driven Acton school and a structured STREAM school produce very different days; decide which suits your child first.
  • Confirm the grade range and open seats. Some Georgia microschools run only elementary grades; others add a hybrid track. Ask which grades have openings.
  • Pin down the real cost. Few schools post tuition, so ask for the full-time and part-time figures, plus any application fee, in writing.
  • Check funding fit. Ask whether the school accepts the Georgia Promise Scholarship and how payment timing works against the quarterly funding.
  • Tour and observe. Watch a normal day, meet the guides, and ask how the school handles reading instruction, conflict, and progress reporting.

If a term like learner-driven or hybrid is new to you, our glossary of terms defines the models plainly. For a deeper walk-through of tours, questions, and red flags, read the complete guide to microschools.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Georgia?

Our directory lists 36 microschools across Georgia, spread over 24 cities. Atlanta holds the largest cluster with 7 listed schools, followed by Duluth, Gainesville, Lawrenceville, Augusta, and Douglasville with 2 each. The models break down to 18 microschools, 10 project-based, 7 learner-driven, and 1 Montessori.

How much do microschools cost in Georgia?

Published tuition across the Georgia microschools in our directory runs from about $35 to roughly $12,000 a year. Most schools do not post a figure online and quote tuition on request. Part-time and hybrid tracks generally cost less than full-time, five-day enrollment.

Does Georgia have an ESA or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Yes. The Georgia Promise Scholarship, passed in 2024, is the state's first education savings account program. Eligible students can receive up to $6,500 a year toward private-school tuition, tutoring, and other approved expenses. Eligibility depends on prior public-school enrollment and attendance zone, so confirm the rules on the state's official page before applying.

How do I choose a microschool in Georgia?

Start with the learning model, learner-driven, project-based, Montessori, or faith-based, then check the grade range, weekly schedule, and cost. Ask whether the school accepts the Georgia Promise Scholarship and which grades have open seats. Tour in person and watch a normal day before you commit.

Are microschools in Georgia legit schools?

Microschools in Georgia operate as small private schools or hybrid homeschool programs, and many are several years old with their own staff and buildings. We verified each featured school's website was live and described an operating program. Ask any school about its legal status, accreditation if relevant, and how it reports student progress.
