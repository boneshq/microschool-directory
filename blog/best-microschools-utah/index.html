The best microschools in Utah at a glance

Our three editors' picks for the best microschools in Utah are Slope School in Provo, a mastery-based program built on six-week quests; Orchard STEM School in Santaquin, a project-based STEM microschool set inside a working orchard; and Pinyon Montessori School in Salt Lake City, a nonprofit Montessori program for ages 3 to 12. Slope School — Provo

Slope School is a mastery-based microschool that opened in Provo in 2019 and now runs campuses in Provo, Millcreek, and Alpine. The model draws on the Acton Academy approach, organizing the year into immersive six-week quests and a "Hero's Journey" framework rather than a fixed lecture schedule. Listed tuition runs from $7,000 to $18,000 per year.

The original Provo campus, at 890 E Quail Valley Drive, caps enrollment at 150 students and serves learners from Pre-K through middle school. Founders Matt and Maria Clayton built the program around mastery-based instruction, where students advance when they show they have learned the material instead of moving on by seat time.

Families looking at Slope School in our directory should confirm which campus has openings and how tuition scales by age, since the price spans a wide band.

Orchard STEM School — Santaquin

Orchard STEM School is a project-based microschool that opened in 2023 inside a fifth-generation family fruit orchard in Santaquin. It serves students in grades K-6 and combines STEM work with nature-based learning, holding classes in the orchard and on frequent local field trips. The program is built around four cornerstones: choice, creativity, competence, and community.

Rather than grade by letter scores or age-based placement, the school documents progress through portfolios, products, and performances. Outdoor work and community service sit alongside the academic projects, which keeps the STEM focus tied to the real orchard the school operates within.

Read the Orchard STEM School listing for contact details. An outdoor, project-based program runs differently from a classroom building, so a visit during the school day helps families judge the fit.

Pinyon Montessori School — Salt Lake City

Pinyon Montessori School is a nonprofit Montessori program in downtown Salt Lake City, at 515 E 100 S, serving children ages 3 to 12. It is a registered 501(c)(3) and is Utah's first Wildflower School, part of a national network of small, teacher-led Montessori microschools. The program describes itself as intentionally small and grounded in community.

The Wildflower model keeps each school small enough for teachers to know every child and run an authentic Montessori classroom. Families with children in the 3-to-12 range can use one school across the early-childhood and elementary years instead of switching programs partway through.

See the full listing for Pinyon Montessori School to schedule a visit. A mixed-age Montessori room works differently from a graded classroom, so tour it before enrolling.

How much do microschools cost in Utah?

Listed microschool tuition in Utah runs from $500 to $18,000 per year, based on the schools in our directory. Price depends on the model, the age of the child, and whether enrollment is part-time or full-time. Acton Academy St. George, for example, lists part-time preschool from $5,100 and full-time enrollment from $10,200 plus a registration fee, while Slope School in Provo spans $7,000 to $18,000.

Utah runs a statewide education savings account, the Utah Fits All Scholarship, worth about $8,000 per year for full-time private-school students. A district court ruled the program unconstitutional in 2025, and it continues uninterrupted only while the Utah Supreme Court hears the appeal, so confirm its current status before counting on it. Families who teach at home instead file a one-time notice of intent with their local school district; the Utah State Board of Education homeschool page publishes the current rules. Ask each school about its own scholarships or aid.

How to choose a microschool in Utah

To choose a microschool in Utah, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or a learner-driven Acton program, to how your child learns best.

Ask how teachers track progress, how many students share a room, and what a typical day looks like. Because most Utah microschools follow the Acton model, compare two or three so you can judge the differences in tuition, schedule, and campus instead of price alone. Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Utah?

microschool.directory lists 9 microschools in Utah across 9 cities, from Logan and Salt Lake City down to St. George. Most follow a learner-driven Acton Academy model, with a Montessori program and project-based microschools rounding out the set. New programs open often, so the count grows through the year.

How much does a microschool cost in Utah?

Listed Utah tuition ranges from $500 to $18,000 per year. The figure depends on the model, the city, and whether enrollment is part-time or full-time. Slope School in Provo, for example, lists $7,000 to $18,000 per year. Always confirm current pricing on the school's own site.

Does Utah have an ESA or scholarship for microschool tuition?

Utah runs the Utah Fits All Scholarship, a state education savings account worth about $8,000 per year for full-time private-school students. A district court ruled the program unconstitutional in 2025, and it continues uninterrupted only while the Utah Supreme Court hears the appeal. Confirm current eligibility and status before counting on it.

How do I choose a microschool in Utah?

Start with the learning model that fits your child, such as Montessori or a learner-driven Acton program. Confirm the grades served, the weekly schedule, and the class size. Tour the school, ask how teachers track progress, and read the enrollment terms. Our parent guide walks through the full checklist.

Are microschools in Utah legit?

Yes. A Utah microschool is a small private school or a homeschool collective. Families who teach at home file a one-time notice of intent with their local school district under state law. Each program we feature has a working website and verified program details, but parents should still confirm licensing and references directly.
