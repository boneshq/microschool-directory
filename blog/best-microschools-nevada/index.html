Acton Academy Red Rock — Las Vegas

Acton Academy Red Rock is a learner-driven microschool in the Las Vegas Valley that follows the Acton Academy model and serves students from kindergarten through twelfth grade. The school groups learners into an Elementary Studio, a Middle School Studio, and a Launchpad Studio, and it is accredited by the Independent Association of Learner-Directed Schools.

Students here work at their own pace on challenging material, earn points for effort and badges for mastery, and collaborate on real-world projects rather than sit through lectures. The school describes learners as being on a "Hero's Journey," with an emphasis on self-management and self-governance. Acton Academy Red Rock is also opening a second learning community at Mt. Rose.

Families looking at Acton Academy Red Rock in our directory should ask for current tuition and grade openings directly, since the site does not post pricing.

Mission Possible Montessori — Enterprise

Mission Possible Montessori is a Montessori program serving the Las Vegas and Henderson area, with a microschool track for elementary and middle-school students. Alongside that microschool, which our listing shows serving grades 1 through 7, it runs Montessori toddler and primary classrooms for younger children, and it lists state license #3070-21.

The Montessori track builds on independence, freedom within limits, and a holistic approach to a child's development. For the elementary and middle microschool, the school focuses on personalized learning that encourages critical thinking and creativity in a small, community-focused setting.

Read the Mission Possible Montessori listing for contact details. Because the program spans toddler through middle-school groups, confirm the exact ages, grades, and weekly schedule for the microschool track before applying.

Acton of the Rubies — Elko

Acton of the Rubies is a nonprofit, learner-driven community in Elko that follows the Acton Academy model and serves students from kindergarten through twelfth grade. The school organizes its program around three pillars: Learning to Learn, Learning to Do, and Learning to Be.

Day to day, students build core academic skills through game-based software and Montessori-style materials, then take on hands-on quests in subjects like robotics, art, and economics, earning learning badges as they master each skill. The program serves homeschooling families across Elko County, and all participating families must comply with Nevada homeschool law.

See the full listing for Acton of the Rubies to schedule a visit. A homeschool-based Acton community runs differently from a conventional private school, so a tour and a clear read of the enrollment terms matter before you commit.

How much do microschools cost in Nevada?

Only one Nevada microschool in our directory publishes tuition: Wilder Nevada (Wildlings) in Las Vegas lists $12,000 a year full-time, $8,400 for three days a week, and $6,000 for two days. Our three picks do not post pricing, and cost depends on the model, the city, the grades served, and the number of days per week. Contact each school directly for current pricing, and ask about any sliding-scale rates or scholarships.

Nevada's only active private-school choice program is the Educational Choice Scholarship, a tax-credit scholarship for low- and middle-income families funded through approved scholarship-granting organizations. A statewide cap on the credit keeps the program small, so funds are limited and not guaranteed. The universal education savings account the legislature passed in 2015 never launched after the Nevada Supreme Court struck down its funding method in 2016, and it is not operational, so families should not count on an ESA. Households that homeschool instead file a notice of intent with their resident school district; the Nevada Department of Education homeschooling office publishes the current rules and form.

How to choose a microschool in Nevada

To choose a microschool in Nevada, start with the learning model, confirm the grades and weekly schedule, tour in person, and read the enrollment terms before paying. Match the model, such as Montessori or Acton learner-driven, to how your child learns best.

Ask how guides track progress, how many students share a studio, and what a typical day looks like. Compare two or three schools so you can judge fit, and because most Nevada listings do not post tuition, get pricing in writing from each one.
