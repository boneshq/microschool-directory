The best microschools in Minnesota at a glance

Three of the best microschools in Minnesota, by our editors' read, are Heart Learning Collective in White Bear Lake (project-based, K-5), Cosmos Montessori in Minneapolis (dual-language Montessori), and Northwoods Forest School in Pine River (forest and nature). Each runs a different model in a different part of the state. microschool.directory lists 16 microschools in Minnesota in total.

Heart Learning Collective — White Bear Lake

Heart Learning Collective is a project-based microschool in White Bear Lake serving kindergarten through fifth grade. The school runs a small K-5 program and recruits new families through demo days and a summer camp, so prospective parents can see the classroom before they enroll.

Heart Learning Collective sits in the northeast Twin Cities metro, which makes it reachable for families in the eastern St. Paul suburbs. A project-based microschool organizes the day around hands-on projects rather than a fixed period-by-period schedule, so the model fits children who learn by building and doing.

Because the program is small and K-5 only, families with a child entering middle school should ask about the path after fifth grade. See the Heart Learning Collective listing for contact details, then sign up for a demo day to confirm the current schedule and cost.

Cosmos Montessori — Minneapolis

Cosmos Montessori is a small Montessori microschool in Minneapolis serving preschool through elementary-aged children. The school runs a dual-language program and describes itself as an intentionally small, community-embedded environment, affiliated with the Minnesota Wildflower Montessori network.

Cosmos Montessori is located at 310 East 38th Street in south Minneapolis. The Montessori method gives each child self-directed work inside a prepared classroom, with mixed-age groups instead of single-grade rooms. The dual-language structure adds a second language to that daily work.

Montessori is the most common model among Minnesota's listed microschools, covering 8 of the 16, so families comparing options will find several Montessori programs in the metro. Check the Cosmos Montessori listing and contact the school for tuition, since the homepage does not publish a rate.

Northwoods Forest School — Pine River

Northwoods Forest School is a forest and nature program in Pine River, in north-central Minnesota. The school is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit that blends Waldorf principles with a Scandinavian, outdoor-first approach, organizing learning around seasonal rhythms, sustainability, and creative work.

Northwoods Forest School is the rural option on this list, well north of the Twin Cities near the Whitefish Chain of Lakes. A forest school holds much of the day outdoors in most weather, which is a deliberate fit for Minnesota's seasons rather than a problem to work around.

Families drawn to outdoor play, hands-on seasonal projects, and a Waldorf-influenced rhythm should review the Northwoods Forest School listing and contact the school about ages served and enrollment, which the homepage does not spell out.

How we chose these Minnesota microschools

We chose these three Minnesota microschools to show variety, not to rank them by score. Each school is listed in our directory, has a working website we checked, and runs a different learning model in a different city. This is an editor's pick, so treat it as a starting shortlist rather than a final verdict.

Our criteria were simple. A school had to appear in the microschool.directory Minnesota listings, have a live website that confirmed it is an operating microschool, and add model or geographic variety to the set. To keep the set useful, the list deliberately avoids three versions of the same brand or three schools in one city.

We did not score teaching quality, test results, or parent satisfaction, because that data is not consistently public across small schools. For terms like Montessori, project-based, or forest school, see our glossary of terms.

How much do microschools cost in Minnesota?

Tuition at Minnesota microschools ranges widely, from low-cost cooperatives to private Montessori programs, and several listed schools do not publish a single annual figure. Many quote a monthly rate or offer part-time and half-day options at a lower price, so the real cost depends on the schedule a family chooses.

Minnesota does not have an education savings account or a private-school voucher, so there is no state account that pays microschool tuition directly. The state instead offers the K-12 Education Credit and Subtraction, which can offset qualifying education expenses for families who meet the income and eligibility rules. Read the program details at the Minnesota Department of Revenue.

Because these programs and each school's rate change from year to year, confirm both the current tuition and your own eligibility before you budget.

How to choose a microschool in Minnesota

To choose a microschool in Minnesota, start with the learning model, then check the ages served, the weekly schedule, and the cost. A Montessori room, a project-based studio, and a forest program each run very differently, so the model decision shapes everything else. Tour in person and ask how the school teaches reading and tracks progress.

Work through a short checklist. Confirm the ages and grades the school serves, the days and hours, the published or quoted tuition, and whether part-time options exist. Ask how the school handles reading instruction, assessment, and a child who is ahead or behind. Ask what happens when a child outgrows the top grade offered.

If any term is unfamiliar, our glossary of terms defines the common models, and our complete guide to microschools walks through how a microschool differs from homeschooling and public school. From there, browse every microschools in Minnesota listing and contact two or three schools that fit your child.

Frequently asked questions

How many microschools are in Minnesota?

microschool.directory lists 16 microschools in Minnesota across 10 cities. Minneapolis has the most with 6 listings. The directory adds schools as editors verify them, so the count grows over time.

How much do microschools cost in Minnesota?

Listed Minnesota tuition varies widely, from low-cost cooperatives to private Montessori programs. Many schools publish a monthly rate rather than a single annual figure, and several offer part-time or half-day options at a lower price. Always confirm the current rate with the school.

Does Minnesota have an ESA or school-choice scholarship for microschools?

Minnesota does not have an education savings account (ESA) or private-school voucher. The state instead offers the K-12 Education Credit and Subtraction through the Department of Revenue, which can offset some education expenses for qualifying families.

How do I choose a microschool in Minnesota?

Start with the learning model, since project-based, Montessori, and forest programs run very differently. Then check the ages served, the weekly schedule, the cost, and the location. Tour in person and ask how the school handles reading instruction and assessment.

Are microschools in Minnesota legit?

Most Minnesota microschools operate as nonpublic schools or homeschool cooperatives under state homeschooling law. They are small by design and not state-accredited like a public school, so parents should verify the legal setup and curriculum directly with each school.
