Microschooling & school choice in Maryland

The directory currently tracks six microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across six Maryland cities, including Baltimore and Bethesda. The model mix leans toward Learner-Driven programs, with three of the six, followed by two Montessori schools and one Project-Based option. Reported tuition starts around $585, though figures vary by program and grade level, so confirm rates with each school. On the funding side, Maryland does not offer a universal education savings account. Its main private-school aid is the BOOST scholarship, which awards tuition help to income-eligible students at participating nonpublic schools, so ask whether a given microschool takes part.

Finding the right fit in Maryland

Start your search in the larger metro areas, where most listings cluster: Baltimore, Bethesda, and the Annapolis-area town of Arnold. Once you have a shortlist, ask each school for its current tuition, since the listed figures shift by grade and program. Maryland families can apply for the BOOST scholarship, which directs state money to income-eligible students at participating nonpublic schools, so check whether the school accepts it and whether you qualify. For broader options or current eligibility rules, the Maryland State Department of Education is the place to confirm details. On a visit, ask how many students share a classroom, how teachers track progress, and how the school handles state testing or umbrella-school paperwork for homeschoolers.

Maryland microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Maryland?

Our directory currently lists six microschools and homeschool co-ops in Maryland, located across six cities from Baltimore to Bethesda. We update listings as new programs open or share details.

How much does a microschool cost in Maryland?

Reported tuition in Maryland starts around $585, but the number varies by program, grade, and schedule. Several schools set their own rates and may offer sibling or need-based discounts, so request a current figure directly.

Can I use a scholarship or ESA for a Maryland microschool?

Maryland does not run a universal education savings account. It does operate the BOOST scholarship, which helps income-eligible families pay tuition at participating nonpublic schools; whether a specific microschool participates is worth confirming before you apply.

What types of microschools are in Maryland?

The listings cover three learning models: Learner-Driven, Montessori, and Project-Based. Three of the six schools use a Learner-Driven approach, including Acton-affiliated programs, while two follow Montessori methods.

How do two Maryland microschools compare?

Acton Academy Annapolis in Arnold and Indigo Montessori in Baltimore show how different two of these schools can look. One sits in the Acton learner-driven network; the other follows a classic Montessori model, so visiting both gives you a useful contrast.
