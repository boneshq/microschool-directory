Microschooling & school choice in Arkansas

The directory covers seven microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across seven Arkansas cities, from Fort Smith and Fayetteville to smaller towns like Brookland and Marshall. Three programs run as general microschools, two use a learner-driven Acton-style approach, and the rest split between Montessori and project-based instruction. Tuition is set school by school, so families should ask each program what it charges per term. Arkansas funds school choice through the LEARNS Act Education Freedom Account, which lets eligible K-12 students put state money toward private tuition, curriculum, and other approved costs. Confirm with the Arkansas Department of Education whether your child qualifies and whether a given school accepts the account.

Finding the right fit in Arkansas

Start in northwest Arkansas, where Fayetteville and Rogers each host an Acton Academy that runs on a learner-driven model. Fort Smith adds a Montessori option at Hollyhock Montessori, and smaller towns like Brookland and DeWitt round out the list. To pay for it, look into the LEARNS Act Education Freedom Account: eligible K-12 students can direct state funds toward private tuition and approved expenses, with the Arkansas Department of Education handling applications and eligibility. On a visit, ask how many students share a classroom, how the school groups ages, and how it reports progress to parents. Also confirm directly with the school whether it accepts Education Freedom Account payments.

Arkansas microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Arkansas?

Seven microschools and homeschool co-ops are listed across Arkansas, located in seven different cities. They range from general microschools to learner-driven academies, a Montessori program, and a project-based school. Most cluster in the northwest part of the state near Fayetteville and Rogers.

How much does a microschool cost in Arkansas?

Tuition is set by each individual school, so the right way to compare costs is to ask programs directly what they charge per year or term. A microschool might run a few thousand dollars annually, but rates differ widely by location and hours. Several Arkansas families offset tuition with state school-choice funds rather than paying entirely out of pocket.

Can you use an Arkansas ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes, in most cases. Arkansas runs the LEARNS Act Education Freedom Account, which lets eligible K-12 students apply state money to private tuition, curriculum, and other approved costs. You apply through the Arkansas Department of Education, and you should confirm a specific school accepts the account before enrolling.

What types of microschools are in Arkansas?

The listings include a mix of models. Three are general microschools, two follow a learner-driven Acton approach such as Acton Academy Fayetteville, one is Montessori at Hollyhock Montessori in Fort Smith, and one is project-based. That spread lets families pick a structure that fits how their child learns.

Where are most of the microschools located in Arkansas?

Northwest Arkansas has the densest options. Fayetteville and Rogers each host an Acton Academy, and Fort Smith adds a Montessori choice, which makes the Interstate 49 corridor the easiest place to compare several programs in a short drive.
