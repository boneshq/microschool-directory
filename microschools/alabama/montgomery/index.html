Microschooling & school choice in Montgomery

This directory lists three microschools and homeschool co-ops in Montgomery. Two run on a Microschool model and one uses a Project-Based approach. Reported tuition starts around $500, though programs set their own rates and some charge more for full-time enrollment. Alabama funds school choice through the CHOOSE Act, a refundable tax-credit education savings account that opened for 2025-26. It provides up to $7,000 per student attending a participating private school and $2,000 per homeschooled student, with a family cap of $4,000 for home learners. Eligibility and the priority order for applicants are set by the Alabama Department of Revenue.

Finding the right fit in Montgomery

Start by comparing the three Montgomery listings side by side, since two follow a Microschool format and one is Project-Based. Reach out to each program for current tuition; the figures here start near $500 but vary with grade level and schedule. If you want public funding, apply for the Alabama CHOOSE Act education savings account through the state, then confirm the school accepts those funds before you enroll. CHOOSE Act money flows to an approved account and reimburses eligible costs like tuition, so keep receipts and check which expenses qualify. On a visit, ask about class size, the daily schedule, how teachers track progress, and what a typical week looks like for a student.

How much does a microschool cost in Montgomery?

Reported tuition in Montgomery starts around $500, but each program sets its own price. Costs often rise with full-time schedules or older grade levels. Ask the specific school for a current rate sheet, since published figures can lag behind what programs actually charge.

What models or types of microschools does Montgomery have?

Montgomery's listings cover two types. Two are Microschools, small multi-age programs with a low student-to-teacher ratio, such as PGF Learning Academy and Primer Microschool - Montgomery. The third, EduPrep Academy, uses a Project-Based model where students learn through hands-on projects rather than a fixed lecture schedule.

Can Montgomery families use an Alabama ESA or scholarship?

Apply through the state and confirm the microschool participates before counting on the funds. Check the current eligibility rules, since income priorities have changed year to year.
