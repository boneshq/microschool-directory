Microschooling & school choice in Alabama

The directory currently tracks 11 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 8 Alabama cities. Montgomery leads with three listings and Birmingham follows with two, while Dothan, Huntsville, Prattville, and Gardendale each carry one. Eight of the schools describe themselves as microschools and three lean on Project-Based learning. Listed tuition starts around $500, though most schools set their own rates and several have not published a figure here. Alabama's CHOOSE Act, a refundable income-tax-credit ESA that started in the 2025-26 year, can put up to $7,000 per child toward a participating school and up to $2,000 per homeschooled student. Families should confirm current eligibility and award amounts with the Alabama Department of Revenue.

Finding the right fit in Alabama

Start your search in Montgomery or Birmingham, where most of the listed programs cluster, then widen out to nearby towns like Prattville or Gardendale if you want a shorter commute. Primer Microschool runs locations in both Dothan and Huntsville, which gives families in those areas a known option to tour first. To stretch your budget, apply for the CHOOSE Act through the Alabama Department of Revenue; the credit can cover tuition at a participating school or eligible homeschool costs, with funds released in stages during the year. Ask each school whether it accepts CHOOSE Act funds, since not every program participates. On a visit, watch how teachers group mixed-age students, check the adult-to-child ratio, and ask how they report progress to parents.

Alabama microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Alabama?

Our directory lists 11 microschools and homeschool co-ops across 8 Alabama cities. Montgomery holds the most with three, and Birmingham has two. The rest are spread through towns such as Dothan, Huntsville, Prattville, and Gardendale.

How much does a microschool cost in Alabama?

Costs vary widely from one program to the next. Listed tuition starts near $500, but many schools set higher rates and some have not posted a number here. Ask each school directly for its current fees and any sibling or sliding-scale discounts.

Can you use an Alabama ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes, in many cases. Alabama's CHOOSE Act is a refundable tax-credit ESA that began funding families in the 2025-26 school year, offering up to $7,000 per child at a participating school and up to $2,000 for a homeschooled student. You apply through the Alabama Department of Revenue, and you should confirm that the specific school accepts these funds.

What types of microschools are in Alabama?

Most of the listings are small microschools, and three describe their approach as Project-Based learning. Some operate as independent campuses, like Res Novae Academy in Pinson, while others belong to a network, such as the Primer Microschool sites in Dothan and Huntsville. Models and grade ranges differ, so compare a few before deciding.

Which Alabama cities have the most microschools?

Birmingham and Montgomery are the strongest starting points, since together they account for five of the 11 listings. Wonderstead in Birmingham and PGF Learning Academy in Montgomery are examples of programs in those two metros. If you live farther out, Primer's Dothan and Huntsville campuses extend the options beyond the Birmingham-Montgomery corridor.
