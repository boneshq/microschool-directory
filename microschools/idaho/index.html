Microschooling & school choice in Idaho

These five programs reach five Idaho cities, from Rexburg in the east to Rathdrum in the north panhandle. Each listing uses a learner-driven approach, so the model mix here is uniform rather than split between teacher-led and self-directed classrooms. Individual schools set their own tuition, and most do not publish rates, so plan to ask directly. On the funding side, Idaho approved a refundable Parental Choice Tax Credit in 2025 worth up to 5,000 dollars per student, which can reimburse tuition, curriculum, and other qualifying education expenses. Boise and Caldwell anchor the southwest cluster of listings.

Finding the right fit in Idaho

Boise is the easiest place to begin, since it sits at the center of the southwest cluster that also includes Caldwell. Families in the north should look at Rathdrum, while Rexburg covers the eastern side of the state. Because Idaho schools rarely post tuition, call each program for a current rate and ask whether siblings get any discount. Idaho's Parental Choice Tax Credit, signed in 2025, lets eligible families claim up to 5,000 dollars per student against private and home education costs, so confirm with the State Tax Commission how to document microschool expenses. On a visit, watch how a Socratic discussion runs, ask how many students share a guide, and find out how the school reports progress to parents.

Idaho microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Idaho?

This directory lists five microschools and homeschool co-ops in Idaho. They sit in five different cities, including Boise, Rexburg, and Rathdrum. The number reflects programs currently catalogued here, not every option statewide, so check back as new schools join.

How much does a microschool cost in Idaho?

Tuition for Idaho microschools is set school by school, and most listed programs do not publish their rates. Expect to request a quote directly, and ask whether the figure covers materials, field trips, and registration fees. The Parental Choice Tax Credit may offset part of what you pay.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Idaho?

Yes. Idaho's Parental Choice Tax Credit, enacted in 2025, gives eligible families a refundable credit of up to 5,000 dollars per student for private and home education expenses, including qualifying microschool costs. Keep receipts and confirm current rules with the Idaho State Tax Commission, since eligibility and funding can change year to year.

What types of microschools are in Idaho?

All five Idaho listings use a learner-driven model, where students set their own goals and work through material at their own pace rather than sitting in lecture-based classes. Several belong to the Acton Academy network, such as Quest Academy North Idaho in Rathdrum and Heroes Academy in Boise.

Why are so many Idaho microschools Acton Academy programs?

Idaho's microschool scene leans heavily on the Acton Academy model, which pairs Socratic discussion with self-paced work and hands-on projects. If that approach appeals to you, Acton Academy Rexburg and Vibrant Life in Caldwell are two listed examples worth touring to see how a learner-driven day actually works.
