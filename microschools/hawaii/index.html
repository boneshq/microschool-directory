Microschooling & school choice in Hawaii

Our Hawaii directory currently covers two microschools across two towns, both on Maui: Kula and Haiku. The model mix splits evenly, with one learner-driven program and one project-based program. Each listing reflects a small enrollment and a multi-age classroom rather than a conventional grade-by-grade structure. Hawaii does not run a statewide private-school voucher or education savings account program, so families generally pay tuition directly or piece together their own funding. Parents who want to offset costs should check the Hawaii Department of Education and individual schools for any scholarships or sliding-scale aid.

Finding the right fit in Hawaii

Start with the two Maui towns where these programs sit: Kula in the Upcountry region and Haiku on the north shore. Reach out to each school directly, since neither posts standardized pricing and rates can change year to year. Hawaii has no statewide ESA or voucher, so ask whether a school offers scholarships, sibling discounts, or a sliding scale tied to income. On a visit, watch how the day is structured, how mixed-age groups work together, and how the school handles reading and math for kids at different levels. Ask about teacher-to-student ratios and how progress gets reported back to you.

Hawaii microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Hawaii?

Our directory lists two microschools and homeschool co-ops in Hawaii, both located on Maui. One sits in Kula and the other in Haiku. Listings change as new programs open, so check back for updates.

How much does a microschool cost in Hawaii?

Our directory does not show a set tuition figure for these Hawaii programs, and rates differ from one school to the next. Microschools often price below traditional private schools because they run lean, but you should contact each program directly for current numbers. Ask whether the quoted rate covers materials, field trips, and extended-day care.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a Hawaii microschool?

Hawaii does not have a statewide education savings account or private-school voucher program as of 2026, so most families pay tuition out of pocket. Some individual schools offer their own scholarships or income-based discounts, so it is worth asking. For the latest on any state aid, check with the Hawaii Department of Education.

What types of microschools are in Hawaii?

Both Hawaii listings use different learning models: one is learner-driven and the other is project-based. A learner-driven program, like Acton Academy Kula, lets students set goals and work at their own pace, while a project-based program centers learning around hands-on projects. Both keep classes small and mix ages.

Are there microschools outside of Maui in Hawaii?

Because both programs sit on Maui, mainland-style commuting between islands is not practical for daily attendance. If you live on Oahu, the Big Island, or Kauai, you may need to look at homeschooling with a co-op or online options instead. Confirm location and schedule before you commit.
