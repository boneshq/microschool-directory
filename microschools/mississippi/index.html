Microschooling & school choice in Mississippi

The directory lists 7 schools across 7 Mississippi cities, including Jackson, Petal, Gulfport, and Pontotoc. Tuition where reported starts around $655, though figures vary by program and grade level. Project-based learning is the most common approach with 3 schools, followed by 2 faith-based programs, one learner-driven Acton academy, and one general microschool. Mississippi runs a Special Needs ESA Program for eligible students with disabilities, but does not offer a universal education savings account, so most families pay tuition directly. Check the Mississippi Department of Education for current eligibility before you assume a program will cover costs.

Finding the right fit in Mississippi

Start your search in the metro areas where listings cluster: Jackson and nearby Hinds County, plus the Gulf Coast around Gulfport. Smaller towns like Petal, Hernando, and Pontotoc also have programs, so widen your radius if nothing fits close to home. Mississippi funds a Special Needs ESA Program for students with qualifying disabilities, but there is no statewide ESA for every family, so budget for out-of-pocket tuition. When you visit a school, ask about class size, how teachers track progress, and whether the calendar matches your work schedule. Request a trial day if the program offers one, and confirm what tuition covers before you sign.

Mississippi microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Mississippi?

Our directory lists 7 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Mississippi, located across 7 cities. They include programs in Jackson, Gulfport, Petal, Hernando, and Pontotoc. New schools open regularly, so check back for updates.

How much does a microschool cost in Mississippi?

Tuition reported in the directory starts around $655, but prices vary widely by school, grade level, and how many days per week a child attends. Some faith-based and co-op programs charge less than full-time academies. Always confirm the current rate and what it includes directly with the school.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a Mississippi microschool?

Mississippi offers a Special Needs ESA Program for students with qualifying disabilities, which can help cover tuition and approved services. There is no universal ESA open to every family in the state right now. Check the Mississippi Department of Education for eligibility rules before counting on funding.

What types of microschools are in Mississippi?

The mix leans toward project-based learning, which describes 3 of the listed schools. You will also find 2 faith-based programs, a learner-driven Acton academy in Pontotoc called Ivy Greene, and one general microschool. Models range from structured to self-directed, so visit a few to see which fits your child.

Which Mississippi cities have the most microschool options?

Jackson and the surrounding Hinds County area have the most options, including Harper Learning Academy and Empowered Prep Academy. The Gulf Coast around Gulfport adds faith-based choices like Kingdom Leadership Academy. If you live outside these hubs, towns such as Petal and Hernando also host programs worth a look.
