Microschooling & school choice in Arizona

The directory covers 20 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 16 Arizona cities. Listed tuition centers around $7,500 per year, though some programs charge less and a few sit higher. The model mix leans toward standalone Microschools, with 10 of them, followed by 5 Project-Based programs, 4 Learner-Driven academies, and a single Montessori option. Queen Creek and Tucson each carry three listings, the largest concentrations in the state. Arizona funds school choice through its Empowerment Scholarship Account, which any K-12 student can use toward private tuition, curriculum, or tutoring.

Finding the right fit in Arizona

Start your search in Queen Creek or Tucson, where the directory shows the most listings, then widen out to nearby cities like Scottsdale or Glendale if you need more options. Arizona's Empowerment Scholarship Account is open to every K-12 student, and once you enroll you draw down the award against approved costs such as tuition, curriculum, and tutoring. Apply through the state Department of Education before you commit to a program, since timing affects when funds become available. On a visit, ask how many students share a classroom, which learning model the school follows, and whether it accepts ESA payments directly. Confirm the current tuition figure too, since the $7,500 reference point shifts by program and grade level.

Arizona microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Arizona?

The directory lists 20 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Arizona, spread across 16 cities. Queen Creek and Tucson hold the most listings with three each. The programs range from standalone microschools to Project-Based and Learner-Driven academies.

How much does a microschool cost in Arizona?

Listed tuition centers around $7,500 per year, though programs vary above and below that figure depending on grade level and schedule. A full-time spot generally costs more than a part-time co-op arrangement. Confirm the current rate directly with any school you contact.

Can you use an Arizona ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes. Arizona's Empowerment Scholarship Account is open to every K-12 student in the state, and families can apply the award toward private tuition, curriculum, or tutoring. You apply through the Arizona Department of Education, and many microschools accept ESA funds.

What types of microschools are in Arizona?

The 20 Arizona listings break down into 10 standalone Microschools, 5 Project-Based programs, 4 Learner-Driven academies, and 1 Montessori school. Learner-Driven options include the Acton Academy locations in Queen Creek and Paradise Valley. Each model sets its own daily structure and group size.

How large are the classes at Arizona microschools?

Most Arizona microschools enroll small groups, often a dozen or two students per room. Schools like Sonoran Learning Collective in Cave Creek and Endeavor Academy in Mesa run mixed-age classrooms, so ask each program how it groups and paces students.
