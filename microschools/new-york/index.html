Microschooling & school choice in New York

The 25 listings here are spread across 23 cities, so most towns carry just one or two programs rather than a dense cluster. The Bronx leads with three sites, and Brooklyn, Buffalo, and Albany each contribute one. Montessori and homeschool co-ops dominate the model mix at eight programs apiece, followed by three microschools, two learner-driven schools, and two project-based ones. Listed tuition starts around $800, though figures shift by grade level and schedule, so confirm the current number with each school. New York has no statewide ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship for private or home education, which means families generally fund these programs themselves.

Finding the right fit in New York

Start your search in the cities with the most options: the Bronx has the largest group of listings, and you will also find programs in Brooklyn, Buffalo, and Albany. Because New York offers no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to pay tuition directly, and ask each school whether it has its own sliding-scale or financial-aid arrangement. Some homeschool co-ops charge per-family or per-session fees rather than a flat annual rate, so request a full fee schedule up front. On a visit, ask about group size, the teacher-to-student ratio, and how the school handles New York's homeschool reporting rules such as the IHIP and quarterly reports. It also helps to ask how a Montessori or learner-driven classroom assesses progress without traditional grades.

New York microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in New York?

Our directory currently lists 25 microschools and homeschool co-ops across New York, covering 23 cities. The Bronx has the most listings, with smaller numbers in places like Brooklyn, Buffalo, and Albany.

How much does a microschool cost in New York?

Tuition listed for New York programs starts around $800, but the real figure depends on the model, grade level, and whether the program runs part-time or full-time. Co-ops often bill per family or per session, so ask each school for its current rate before you compare.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship to pay for a microschool in New York?

New York does not offer a statewide ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship program, so most families pay these tuition costs out of pocket. Individual schools may offer their own financial aid or sliding-scale fees, so it is worth asking directly.

What types of microschools are in New York?

The mix leans toward Montessori and homeschool co-ops, with eight of each, plus a few microschools, learner-driven programs, and project-based schools.

What does New York require from homeschooling families?

New York requires home-instructed students to file an Individualized Home Instruction Plan and submit quarterly reports and an annual assessment, so ask any co-op or microschool how it supports those filings.
