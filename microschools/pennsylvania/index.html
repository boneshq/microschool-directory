Microschooling & school choice in Pennsylvania

The directory covers 15 schools spread across 10 Pennsylvania cities. Philadelphia holds the largest share with six listings, while smaller towns like Glen Mills and Lancaster each contribute a program. Montessori is the most common approach with six schools, followed by five learner-driven Acton Academy campuses and three independent microschools. Tuition starts around $800, though most schools set their own rates and many adjust by grade or family income. Pennsylvania has no statewide ESA, but the EITC and OSTC tax-credit scholarship programs can help eligible families cover private-school tuition.

Finding the right fit in Pennsylvania

Start your search in Philadelphia, which has the most options, then widen to nearby towns such as Glen Mills or Wexford if you want a learner-driven Acton Academy campus. Pennsylvania does not run a statewide ESA, so funding usually comes through the EITC or OSTC tax-credit scholarship programs, which route donor-funded awards to families through approved scholarship organizations. Apply early, since these awards are capped each year and tend to run out. Ask each school whether it partners with a scholarship organization that accepts EITC or OSTC money. On a visit, ask about group size, how mixed-age classrooms are handled, and what a typical day looks like. Request the current tuition schedule in writing, since posted rates often shift by grade.

Pennsylvania microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Pennsylvania?

This directory lists 15 microschools and homeschool co-ops across Pennsylvania, spread over 10 cities. Philadelphia has the most, with six listings. The rest sit in towns like Glen Mills, Wexford, Schwenksville, and Lancaster.

How much does a microschool cost in Pennsylvania?

Tuition starts near $800, but each school sets its own price and many adjust by grade or household income. Some programs charge well above the entry figure, so request a written tuition schedule before you commit. A few schools also offer sibling discounts.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania has no statewide ESA. Families instead rely on the EITC and OSTC tax-credit scholarship programs, which fund private-school tuition through approved scholarship organizations. Confirm with each school whether it works with an organization that accepts these awards, and apply early because the funds are capped annually.

What types of microschools are in Pennsylvania?

The listings lean toward Montessori and learner-driven models. Six schools use Montessori, five run as Acton Academy campuses such as Acton Academy Pittsburgh in Wexford, and three are independent microschools. One program blends arts with a learner-driven approach. Most families choose between a structured Montessori setting and the project-based Acton model.

How are microschools classified under Pennsylvania law?

Pennsylvania recognizes microschools as either private schools or homeschool arrangements depending on how they register. A good example is Hyacinth Montessori in Philadelphia, which serves the city core. That distinction affects which scholarship money applies, so ask how a school is classified with the state before enrolling.
