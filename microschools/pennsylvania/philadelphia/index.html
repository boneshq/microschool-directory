Microschooling & school choice in Philadelphia

The directory lists six microschools and homeschool co-ops in Philadelphia. Five use the Montessori method, including Hyacinth Montessori and Clover Montessori, while Freedom Arts Academy runs an arts-focused, learner-driven program. Listed tuition starts around $800, though each school sets its own rates and many charge more for full-day or older grades. Pennsylvania has no statewide education savings account that pays for private tuition or homeschooling. The state does fund two tax-credit scholarship programs, EITC and OSTC, which route donated funds to families through approved scholarship organizations. Eligibility and award amounts depend on household income, so confirm details with each school and the scholarship organization.

Finding the right fit in Philadelphia

Note the published tuition, which begins near $800, then call each school to get full pricing for your child's age and schedule. Pennsylvania routes private-school aid through the EITC and OSTC tax-credit scholarship programs rather than a direct ESA, so ask whether a school works with a scholarship organization and how income limits apply. On a visit, ask about the daily schedule, group sizes, how teachers track progress, and how the school handles a child who is ahead or behind grade level. Bring any homeschool umbrella or evaluation paperwork your family already uses so the school can tell you how it fits their model.

Philadelphia microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Philadelphia?

The directory lists six microschools and homeschool co-ops in Philadelphia. Five run on the Montessori method and one, Freedom Arts Academy, uses an arts-focused, learner-driven approach. Each program is independent, so size, hours, and grade ranges differ. Contact the schools directly to confirm current openings.

How much does a microschool cost in Philadelphia?

Listed tuition in Philadelphia starts around $800, but that figure is a starting point rather than a fixed price. Schools set their own rates and often charge more for full-day attendance or older grades. Ask each provider for a full fee schedule, including any registration or materials costs.

What learning models do Philadelphia microschools use?

Five of the six listings are Montessori programs, such as Our Sun Montessori and Mountain Laurel Montessori, which use mixed-age classrooms and hands-on materials. The sixth, Freedom Arts Academy, runs an arts-focused, learner-driven model. Visiting more than one lets you compare how each approach fits your child.

Can Philadelphia families use a Pennsylvania ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Pennsylvania does not offer a statewide education savings account for tuition or homeschool costs. It does run the EITC and OSTC tax-credit scholarship programs, which fund awards through approved scholarship organizations based on household income. Ask each school whether it partners with one of these organizations and how to apply.

How do I enroll my child in a Philadelphia microschool?

To enroll, reach out to the specific Philadelphia school you like and ask about its application window and any waitlist. Many microschools admit small cohorts, so spots can fill early in the year. If the six listed programs are full or far from you, check nearby Pennsylvania towns in the directory for additional options.
