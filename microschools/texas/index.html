Microschooling & school choice in Texas

The 102 listed schools span 54 Texas cities, with tuition running from $1,200 to $15,500 a year. Austin leads with 14 schools, followed by Houston with 8 and San Antonio with 7; Cedar Park, Dripping Springs, and Fort Worth round out the busiest areas. Learner-Driven academies (38) and microschools (37) make up most listings, with 21 Project-Based programs and a handful of Montessori options. Texas families can apply for the state Education Savings Account, created by Senate Bill 2 in 2025, which begins funding eligible students for the 2026-27 school year. Award amounts and eligibility are set by the Texas Comptroller, so confirm current rules before you enroll.

Finding the right fit in Texas

Start in the metro areas with the most options: Austin has 14 listed schools, and Houston and San Antonio each carry several more. Smaller hubs like Cedar Park and Dripping Springs are worth a look if you are west or south of Austin. Texas now runs an Education Savings Account under Senate Bill 2, so check the Texas Comptroller's site for application windows, award amounts, and which expenses qualify before you commit. On a visit, ask how many students share a guide, how the school handles different grade levels in one room, and whether it accepts ESA funds. Tuition across the directory ranges from $1,200 to $15,500, so request the full fee schedule including any registration or materials charges.

Texas microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Texas?

This directory lists 102 microschools and homeschool co-ops across Texas. They sit in 54 cities, with Austin holding the most at 14 listings. New schools are added as they are verified, so the count grows over time.

How much does a microschool cost in Texas?

Listed tuition runs from $1,200 to $15,500 a year, depending on the program, location, and number of days per week. Learner-Driven academies and small microschools often fall in the middle of that range. Always ask for the full fee schedule, since registration and materials fees can sit on top of base tuition.

Can you use a Texas ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes, Texas created an Education Savings Account through Senate Bill 2 in 2025, with funding available for the 2026-27 school year. The Texas Comptroller administers the program and sets award amounts and eligibility, and not every family or expense qualifies. Check the Comptroller's site for application windows and confirm with the school that it accepts ESA funds.

What types of microschools are in Texas?

Most Texas listings are Learner-Driven academies (38) or independent microschools (37), with 21 Project-Based programs, a few Montessori options, and one Forest or Nature school. Acton Academy Austin Main Campus is one example of the Learner-Driven model, where students set goals and work at their own pace. Visiting a couple of different types helps you see which structure fits your child.

Which Texas cities have the most microschools?

Austin is the busiest single hub with 14 listed schools, including Acton Academy Austin Main Campus. Houston and San Antonio follow with 8 and 7, and growing pockets around Cedar Park, Dripping Springs, and Fort Worth give families options outside the largest metros. If you are near Katy, Mirus Academy is another listed microschool to consider.
