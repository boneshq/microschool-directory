Microschooling & school choice in Houston

This directory tracks eight microschools and homeschool co-ops across Houston. The four learning models are represented in equal measure: two Montessori programs, two learner-driven, two project-based, and two listed simply as microschools. Tuition for the programs we have pricing on runs around $15,500 per year, though smaller co-ops and part-time options often cost less. Texas families gained a new funding route in 2025, when Senate Bill 2 created Education Freedom Accounts. Those accounts begin distributing money for the 2026-27 school year, with roughly $10,000 available per student at an accredited private school and smaller amounts set aside for homeschoolers.

Finding the right fit in Houston

Start by comparing the eight Houston listings side by side, since the models differ a lot in daily structure. A Montessori room like Yellow Rose Montessori runs very differently from a learner-driven studio such as Acton Academy Houston Heights, so think about which fits your child. With Education Freedom Accounts arriving for the 2026-27 year, ask each school whether it plans to accept the funds and how it handles the paperwork. The Texas Education Agency posts current eligibility rules and the per-student amounts, so check there before you budget. On a visit, ask about group size, how teachers track progress, the daily schedule, and what a typical week looks like. Request to sit in on a class if the school allows it.

Houston microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Houston?

Eight microschools and homeschool co-ops in Houston are listed in this directory. They span four learning models, with two programs each in the Montessori, learner-driven, project-based, and microschool categories. Names range from Acton Academy Houston Heights to Elderflower Montessori.

How much does a microschool cost in Houston?

Tuition varies by model and schedule. Among the Houston programs we have pricing for, the figure lands around $15,500 a year for a full-time spot. Part-time co-ops and smaller programs frequently charge less, so confirm the current rate directly with each school.

What types of microschools does Houston have?

Houston covers four model types. Two schools follow Montessori, two are learner-driven, two run project-based programs, and two describe themselves as microschools. Montessori options include Yellow Rose Montessori and Elderflower Montessori, while Acton Academy Houston Heights represents the learner-driven approach.

Can Houston families use a Texas ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes, in most cases. Texas created Education Freedom Accounts under Senate Bill 2 in 2025, and the money begins flowing for the 2026-27 school year. Eligible families can put roughly $10,000 per student toward an accredited private school, with smaller amounts for homeschoolers. Confirm with the Texas Education Agency and the school that your chosen program qualifies.

How do I enroll my child in a Houston microschool?

Reach out to each school directly, since most Houston microschools enroll on a rolling basis and keep seats limited. Some, like Acton Academy Houston Heights, use an application and interview process, while smaller programs may simply ask for a visit and a deposit. If a school you like is full, ask about its waitlist and whether nearby campuses, such as Acton Leadership Academy Champions/Klein, have openings.
