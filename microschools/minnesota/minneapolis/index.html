Microschooling & school choice in Minneapolis

Six microschools and homeschool co-ops in Minneapolis appear in this directory. All six use the Montessori model, including Cosmos Montessori, Lirio Montessori, and Minnesota Wildflower Montessori Charter School. Listed tuition figures were not reported for these programs, so contact each school for its current rate; Montessori microschools in Minnesota commonly run a few thousand dollars a year for part-time spots up to roughly full private-school pricing for full days. Minnesota has no broad ESA or voucher program, so public dollars do not directly follow a child into a private microschool. The state does offer the K-12 Education Credit and the larger Education Subtraction, which let families recover some qualifying education costs at tax time. Charter options such as Minnesota Wildflower are tuition-free public schools, a separate path from the private listings here.

Finding the right fit in Minneapolis

Start by comparing the six Minneapolis listings side by side, since all follow the Montessori method but differ in age range, schedule, and full- versus part-time options. Reach out to two or three that fit your child's age and your commute, and ask for a tour. Because Minnesota has no voucher or ESA, plan to fund tuition yourself, then check whether the K-12 Education Credit or Education Subtraction can offset some costs when you file. On a visit, ask about class size, the lead teacher's Montessori training, the daily rhythm, and how the school tracks progress. Confirm the real all-in cost, including materials and any care outside core hours. Note too that one listing, Minnesota Wildflower Montessori, is a tuition-free public charter, which changes both the application process and the price.

Minneapolis microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Minneapolis?

This directory lists six microschools and homeschool co-ops in Minneapolis. All six are Montessori programs, among them Cosmos Montessori, Lirio Montessori, Rosebay Montessori, Starflower Montessori, and Sweet Pea Montessori. That makes Minneapolis one of Minnesota's larger clusters for this style of small-classroom school.

How much does a Minneapolis microschool cost?

Tuition was not reported for the Minneapolis listings, so ask each school for its current rate. Private Montessori microschools in the area often charge a few thousand dollars a year for part-time enrollment and more for full-day spots, while one listing is a tuition-free public charter. Confirm the all-in figure, including materials and extended care.

What models do these schools use?

Every Minneapolis program in this directory uses the Montessori method, with mixed-age classrooms, hands-on materials, and child-led, self-paced work. Some run as private microschools and one, Minnesota Wildflower Montessori, is a public charter school. The directory does not list other co-op styles in this city.

Can I use a state ESA or scholarship in Minnesota?

Minnesota does not have a statewide ESA or private-school voucher, so public funds will not directly cover private microschool tuition. The state does provide the K-12 Education Credit and Education Subtraction, which can offset some qualifying costs at tax time, subject to income limits and rules. Check the Minnesota Department of Education and the Department of Revenue for current eligibility.

How do I enroll my child in a Minneapolis program?

Begin by contacting the specific schools that fit your child's age and your part of the city, such as Starflower Montessori or Sweet Pea Montessori. Ask about openings, waitlists, and start dates, since small programs fill quickly. For the public charter option, Minnesota Wildflower Montessori, look into the separate enrollment or lottery process rather than a private admissions path.
