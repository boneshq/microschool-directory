Microschooling & school choice in Minnesota

The 16 listed programs span 10 Minnesota cities. Minneapolis holds the most with six, and Moorhead follows with two, while Saint Paul, Apple Valley, and Stewartville each have one. Montessori is the dominant model at eight schools, followed by three learner-driven programs and three that identify simply as microschools, plus a project-based option and a forest school. Many of these programs do not publish a flat tuition figure, so families confirm rates directly with each school. Minnesota has no statewide school voucher or education savings account, but the K-12 Education Credit and Subtraction can reduce the cost of qualifying instructional expenses for eligible households.

Finding the right fit in Minnesota

Start where the listings cluster: Minneapolis carries six of the 16 programs, and Moorhead in the northwest has two more. Parents in Saint Paul, Apple Valley, or Stewartville will find single options closer to home. Minnesota does not fund a voucher or ESA, so plan to pay tuition out of pocket, then claim the K-12 Education Credit or Subtraction at tax time if your household qualifies and the expenses are eligible. On a visit, ask how a school handles state attendance and reporting requirements, what a typical day looks like, the student-to-teacher ratio, and how a Montessori or learner-driven classroom adapts to a child who is ahead or behind grade level.

Minnesota microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Minnesota?

The directory lists 16 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Minnesota, spread across 10 cities. Minneapolis has the largest concentration at six, with Moorhead next at two. The rest are single listings in cities like Saint Paul, Apple Valley, and Stewartville.

How much does a microschool cost in Minnesota?

Costs vary widely by program and model. Many Minnesota microschools set rates per family or per program rather than posting a single tuition number, so the most reliable figure comes from contacting the school directly. Ask whether the quoted price covers materials, meals, and any part-time options.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a Minnesota microschool?

Minnesota does not run a statewide voucher or education savings account program. Households can instead use the state K-12 Education Credit and Subtraction to lower the cost of qualifying instructional expenses, depending on income and the type of expense. Check with the Minnesota Department of Revenue and the school for what currently qualifies.

What types of microschools does Minnesota have?

The mix leans heavily Montessori, which accounts for eight of the listed programs. There are also three learner-driven schools, three that describe themselves simply as microschools, one project-based program, and a forest school. Cosmos Montessori in Minneapolis and Northwoods Forest School in Pine River show the range. Rochester has Forward Leap and Moorhead has Odyssey: An Acton Academy, both microschools.

Are there microschools outside the Twin Cities?

Yes. Several listings sit outside the Twin Cities, so families in greater Minnesota have options without commuting into the metro. Burnsville, White Bear Lake, and Pine River all appear among the sample schools.
