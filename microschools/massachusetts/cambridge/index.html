Microschooling & school choice in Cambridge

This directory lists 10 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Cambridge, all of them Montessori programs. Tuition runs from $19,500 to $30,172 a year, which reflects the higher cost of full-day independent schooling in the Boston metro. Massachusetts does not currently offer a statewide ESA or school-voucher program, so most families here pay tuition directly or rely on individual school financial aid. The state does allow homeschooling under local school-committee approval, which gives co-op families a recognized path. Parents weighing these schools should check each program's aid policy, since published tuition is often negotiable for siblings or need-based cases.

Finding the right fit in Cambridge

Start by comparing the 10 Cambridge listings on the details that matter to your family: age range, daily schedule, class size, and where each campus sits relative to your commute. Because every school here follows the Montessori method, ask how strictly each one applies it and how mixed-age classrooms are grouped. Massachusetts has no statewide ESA, so budget around the $19,500 to $30,172 tuition band and ask each school about sibling discounts or need-based aid. If you plan to homeschool through a co-op, contact your local school committee early, since approval happens at the district level. On a visit, watch a full work period rather than a staged tour, and ask about teacher Montessori certification and staff turnover. Request the most recent tuition agreement in writing before you commit.

Cambridge microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Cambridge?

This directory lists 10 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Cambridge, Massachusetts. All 10 are Montessori programs, ranging from toddler and primary classrooms through elementary years. Cambridge is one of the more concentrated Montessori markets in the Boston area.

How much does a microschool cost in Cambridge?

Tuition for these Cambridge schools runs from $19,500 to $30,172 per year. The figure depends on the age of the child, the length of the day, and whether the program is half-day or full-day. Several schools offer need-based aid or sibling discounts, so the published number is not always the final cost.

What types of microschools are in Cambridge?

Every school currently listed in Cambridge follows the Montessori method, including mixed-age classrooms and child-directed work periods. You will find toddler, primary, and elementary Montessori settings among the listings. Allium Montessori and Wildflower Montessori are two examples on this directory.

Can I use a state ESA or scholarship in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts does not have a statewide ESA or voucher program as of 2026, so there is no public account you can apply this tuition to. Families generally pay directly or use a school's own financial aid. For current eligibility, check with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.

How do I enroll in a Cambridge microschool?

To enroll, contact the schools directly, since Montessori programs in Cambridge often have waitlists for primary classrooms. Many open enrollment in winter for the following fall, so reach out well ahead. Schools such as Snapdragon Montessori School and Aster Montessori can tell you about current openings and visit days.
