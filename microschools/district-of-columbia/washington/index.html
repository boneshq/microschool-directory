Microschooling & school choice in Washington

Five microschools and homeschool co-ops in Washington appear in this directory. Three follow a Montessori approach, with one microschool and one learner-driven program rounding out the mix. Tuition figures are not posted here, so families should ask each program directly for current rates. For funding, D.C. families can look at the federally funded D.C. Opportunity Scholarship Program, which awards income-based vouchers for tuition at participating private schools. Award amounts adjust each year and are capped by grade level, so confirm eligibility and current figures with the program before enrolling.

Finding the right fit in Washington

Start by comparing the five Washington listings side by side: Mysa School runs as a microschool, The Riverseed School and Blue Montessori use a Montessori method, and ANANSI Academy follows a learner-driven model. Make a short list of which approach fits your child, then reach out to two or three programs for current tuition since rates are not posted here. If cost is a concern, check whether your household qualifies for the D.C. Opportunity Scholarship Program, which provides income-based vouchers toward private school tuition. On a visit, ask about group size, the daily schedule, how teachers track progress, and how the school handles children at different grade levels. Bring questions about enrollment timelines and any waitlists, since smaller programs fill quickly.

Washington microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Washington?

This directory lists five microschools and homeschool co-ops in Washington, D.C. Among them are three Montessori programs, one microschool, and one learner-driven school. That gives families a few distinct teaching styles to compare within the District.

How much does a microschool cost in Washington?

Tuition is not posted in this directory, so the most reliable way to get a number is to contact each program directly. Costs differ by model, hours, and grade level, and several of these schools are small private settings. Ask whether the quoted rate covers materials, meals, or extended-day care so you can compare programs fairly.

What models or types of microschools are in Washington?

Five Washington listings cover three approaches. Three are Montessori schools, including The Riverseed School and Blue Montessori, while Mysa School runs as a microschool and ANANSI Academy uses a learner-driven model. Each approach handles pacing and mixed-age groups differently, so a visit helps.

Can Washington families use a D.C. scholarship for a microschool?

Washington runs the federally funded D.C. Opportunity Scholarship Program, which gives income-eligible families vouchers toward private school tuition. Whether a given microschool participates depends on the school, so confirm acceptance and current award amounts before you enroll. Award figures adjust each year, so use official sources for the latest numbers.

How do I enroll my child at a Washington microschool?

To enroll, contact the Washington programs that fit your child and ask about open seats, since small schools often keep waitlists. If none of the five has space, ask each one to point you toward nearby options or similar programs just outside the city. Starting the conversation early gives you the best shot at a spot for the coming term.
