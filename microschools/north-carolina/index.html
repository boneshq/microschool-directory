Microschooling & school choice in North Carolina

The directory covers 15 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 13 North Carolina cities. Cary holds the largest cluster with three schools, and listings reach west to Asheville. Reported tuition runs from $557 to $15,000 a year, so the price spread is wide and depends heavily on how many days a week a child attends. Montessori and learner-driven programs are the two most common models, with several project-based schools and a couple of general microschools rounding out the mix. On the funding side, North Carolina opened its Opportunity Scholarship to all income levels in 2024, and the ESA+ program adds separate accounts for students with disabilities.

Finding the right fit in North Carolina

Start your search in Cary, which has the most listings, or look toward Asheville and Davidson if you are in the western part of the state. North Carolina's Opportunity Scholarship is open to families at any income level and can cover private school tuition, with award amounts that scale by household income. If your child has a documented disability, the ESA+ program provides a separate account for tutoring, therapy, and curriculum. Apply through the state Education Assistance Authority, since both programs run on application windows rather than rolling enrollment. When you visit a school, ask how many children are in each group, what a typical day looks like, and whether they accept scholarship payments directly.

North Carolina microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in North Carolina?

This directory lists 15 microschools and homeschool co-ops in North Carolina across 13 cities. Cary has the most, with three schools, and the rest are scattered from Asheville in the west to towns like Holly Ridge near the coast. The count grows as new programs register, so check back if your area shows nothing today.

How much does a microschool cost in North Carolina?

Listed tuition in North Carolina runs from $557 to $15,000 per year. The low end usually reflects part-time co-ops or a few days a week, while full-time five-day programs sit toward the higher end. Ask each school exactly what its fee covers and whether materials or testing cost extra.

Can you use a North Carolina ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes. North Carolina's Opportunity Scholarship became available to all income levels in 2024 and can be applied to private school tuition, including many microschools that operate as private schools. Families of students with disabilities can also use the ESA+ program for tutoring, therapy, and curriculum. Confirm with the school that it is registered to accept these funds before you enroll.

What types of microschools are in North Carolina?

The listed schools use four main models: Montessori and learner-driven are the most common, followed by project-based programs and a smaller number of general microschools. Acton Academy Davidson runs a learner-driven model, while Hawthorn Montessori in Asheville follows the Montessori approach. Visiting two or three lets you compare how structured each day actually is.

Where are North Carolina's microschools concentrated?

Cary leads with three listings, and the remaining schools are spread thin across the state, including Davidson, Indian Trail, and Stokesdale. Because most cities have just one option, many families end up driving or considering a part-time co-op alongside homeschooling.
