Microschooling & school choice in Chicago

Our Chicago listings include three schools. Two of them describe themselves as learner-driven, where students set goals and move at their own pace, while the third operates as a microschool with a small mixed-age group. Programs in this format usually enroll well under 50 students, which keeps class sizes low and lets teachers adjust the day around each child. Tuition at independent microschools in the Chicago area varies widely by hours, ages served, and whether the program runs full-time or as a part-week co-op. Illinois ended its Invest in Kids tax-credit scholarship in 2023 and has not replaced it, so there is no active statewide voucher or ESA for private tuition right now. Check the Illinois State Board of Education for any current scholarship or assistance options before you budget.

Finding the right fit in Chicago

Start by opening the three Chicago listings and noting which two are learner-driven and which one is the structured microschool, then match that against how your child works best. Request a tour or trial day at each, and watch how teachers handle a range of ages and abilities in one room. Ask about the daily schedule, the student-to-teacher ratio, how academic progress gets measured, and what happens when a child is ahead or behind grade level. Because Illinois has no active statewide ESA or voucher, ask each school directly about payment plans, sibling discounts, or any private scholarship funds it offers. It also helps to confirm the program's calendar, drop-off hours, and whether it can meet any IEP or special-needs requirements your child has.

Chicago microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Chicago?

Our directory lists three microschools and learner-driven programs in Chicago. Two use a learner-driven approach and one runs as a structured microschool. We update listings as new programs open, so it is worth checking back if none of the three fit.

How much does a Chicago microschool cost?

Cost depends on the program, since independent microschools in Chicago set their own tuition based on hours, ages served, and full-time versus part-week schedules. Learner-driven programs and co-ops often price differently from a five-day microschool. Ask each school for its current tuition and any payment plans, because published rates change year to year.

What types of microschools are listed in Chicago?

The Chicago listings split into two formats. Two schools, including The Sanctuary Academy, follow a learner-driven model where students set their own goals and pace. The third, Midwest Academy for Gifted Education, runs as a microschool with a small mixed-age group.

Can I use an Illinois ESA or scholarship to pay tuition?

Not right now. Illinois let its Invest in Kids tax-credit scholarship expire at the end of 2023 and has not created a replacement, so there is no active statewide ESA or voucher for private tuition. Check the Illinois State Board of Education for any newer assistance before you assume a program is unaffordable.

How do I enroll, and what if none of the three fit?

Reach out to each school directly to ask about open seats, since small programs like Wander Through Wonder Academy fill limited spots fast. Many run rolling admissions and may keep a waitlist. If the three Chicago options do not fit, widen your search to nearby suburbs, where more microschools and co-ops operate.
