Microschooling & school choice in Illinois

The directory currently tracks 10 microschools and homeschool co-ops across 8 Illinois cities. Chicago holds three of the listings, with single programs in suburbs including Burr Ridge, Deerfield, Barrington, and Palatine, plus a downstate option in Canton. The model mix is balanced: four learner-driven programs, four independent microschools, and two Montessori campuses. Published tuition figures are not yet collected for these listings, so families should request current rates directly from each school. On funding, Illinois no longer runs a statewide private-school-choice program; the Invest in Kids tax-credit scholarship sunset at the end of 2023 and was not renewed, so check the Illinois State Board of Education for any current options.

Finding the right fit in Illinois

Chicago is the practical place to begin, since it carries the most listings and the widest range of models. Suburban families can look at Burr Ridge, Deerfield, Barrington, or Palatine, while Canton covers the central part of the state. Because Illinois currently has no statewide ESA or voucher program, plan to pay tuition directly and ask each school whether it offers payment plans or need-based discounts. When you visit, ask about group sizes, the daily schedule, and how a learner-driven or Montessori classroom handles different ages and skill levels. Request the current tuition and any registration fees in writing, since the directory does not yet publish those figures. Confirm the school's approach to state homeschool reporting so your paperwork stays in order.

Illinois microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Illinois?

The directory lists 10 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Illinois, spread across 8 cities. Chicago has the most with three listings, and the remaining programs sit in suburbs and one downstate town. New programs open regularly, so the count can grow over time.

How much does a microschool cost in Illinois?

Tuition figures are not yet collected for the Illinois listings, so there is no single published price to quote. Costs differ widely between a small learner-driven program and a Montessori campus, and some schools offer payment plans or need-based aid. Contact each school directly for its current rate and any registration fees.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Illinois?

Illinois does not currently fund a statewide ESA or voucher you can apply to private microschool tuition. The Invest in Kids tax-credit scholarship, which once helped some families, ended after 2023 and was not renewed. For the latest on any replacement program, check with the Illinois State Board of Education before you budget.

What types of microschools does Illinois have?

The Illinois listings break down into three approaches: four learner-driven programs, four independent microschools, and two Montessori campuses. Learner-driven sites such as Acton Academy Barrington give students more say over how they pace their work, while Guidepost Montessori at Burr Ridge follows the Montessori method. Visiting a few lets you compare the daily rhythm of each.

Are there microschools in Illinois outside the Chicago area?

Most of the listings cluster in the Chicago area, but the directory does reach beyond it. Canton Learning Collaborative serves the central part of the state, so families outside the suburbs still have an option. If nothing nearby fits, ask listed schools whether they run hybrid or part-time co-op days that cut down on travel.
