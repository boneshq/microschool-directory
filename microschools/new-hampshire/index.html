Microschooling & school choice in New Hampshire

Two listings cover two New Hampshire towns: Henniker and Hillsborough. One school, Acton Academy Merrimack Valley in Henniker, follows a Learner-Driven approach, while Happy Hens Farm & Forest School in Hillsborough runs a Forest or Nature program built around outdoor time. The packet does not carry a tuition figure for either, so plan to ask each school what it charges and what a typical year costs. New Hampshire funds school choice through the Education Freedom Account program, an ESA that deposits state per-pupil money into an account families control. Eligible families can apply EFA funds toward microschool tuition, curriculum, and other approved expenses. Both towns sit close to Concord, which makes day trips to compare programs realistic.

Finding the right fit in New Hampshire

Start with the two towns that hold listings: Henniker, home to Acton Academy Merrimack Valley, and Hillsborough, where Happy Hens Farm & Forest School operates. Both sit near Concord and Manchester, so a parent can tour both in a single day. To pay for it, look at the Education Freedom Account program: New Hampshire deposits state per-pupil funds into an account, and eligible families spend it on tuition and approved learning costs through an online portal. Check the current income threshold and application window on the state EFA page before you commit. On a visit, ask how many students attend, what a normal day looks like, how the school handles different ages, and whether it accepts EFA payments. Request the full cost in writing, since neither listing publishes a rate here.

New Hampshire microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in New Hampshire?

The directory lists two microschools and homeschool co-ops in New Hampshire, located in Henniker and Hillsborough. Both towns are within driving distance of Concord and Manchester. The directory continues to add programs as they open.

How much does a microschool cost in New Hampshire?

Neither New Hampshire listing publishes a set tuition rate, so cost depends on the school. Small programs in the region commonly fall in the few-thousand to low-five-figure range per year, but you should confirm directly with Acton Academy Merrimack Valley or Happy Hens Farm & Forest School. Ask whether fees cover materials, meals, or field trips.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in New Hampshire?

Yes. New Hampshire runs the Education Freedom Account program, an ESA that places state per-pupil money into a family-controlled account. Eligible families can use those funds for microschool tuition and other approved expenses, subject to the current income rules on the state EFA page.

What types of microschools does New Hampshire have?

The two listings split between a Learner-Driven model at Acton Academy Merrimack Valley and a Forest or Nature model at Happy Hens Farm & Forest School. Learner-driven programs let students set goals and work at their own pace, while forest and nature programs center outdoor, hands-on learning.

Does New Hampshire require microschools to register with the state?

Microschools often operate as private schools or under homeschool rules, and requirements differ by setup. Ask each program how it is organized and how it reports to families, then confirm any state filing on the New Hampshire Department of Education site.
