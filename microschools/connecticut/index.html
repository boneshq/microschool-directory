Connecticut lists two microschools, both Montessori programs in New Haven. These are small, independent learning communities rather than large private schools, and each sets its own enrollment terms. Connecticut does not run a statewide private school choice program, so most families pay tuition directly or combine it with homeschooling.

Microschooling & school choice in Connecticut

Both of Connecticut's listed microschools sit in New Haven, and both follow a Montessori approach. The two programs are The Dream Academy of CT and Sankofa Learning Center. Individual schools set their own tuition, so contact each one directly for current pricing. Connecticut does not offer a statewide education savings account or voucher, and its public options for choice are limited to charter and magnet schools. Parents who want public funding usually look at those routes, while microschool families typically pay out of pocket or fold the cost into a homeschool budget.

Finding the right fit in Connecticut

Start in New Haven, where both listed Montessori programs operate. Reach out to The Dream Academy of CT and Sankofa Learning Center to ask about openings, age ranges, and daily schedule. Because Connecticut has no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to cover tuition yourself or check with your local district about magnet and charter alternatives. On a visit, ask how many children are in each classroom, how the school handles mixed ages, and what a typical day looks like. Confirm tuition and any sibling or sliding-scale options before you commit.

Connecticut microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Connecticut?

This directory lists two microschools in Connecticut, and both are in New Haven. Each follows a Montessori model. The directory grows as new programs are added, so check back if you don't see a fit near you right now.

How much does a microschool cost in Connecticut?

Tuition is set by each individual school rather than a statewide rate. The two listed programs do not publish standard pricing here, so contact them directly for figures. Many microschools offer sibling discounts or payment plans, so ask about those when you call.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Connecticut?

Connecticut does not run a statewide private school choice program such as an education savings account or voucher. That means you generally cannot apply public scholarship money toward these microschools. Check the Connecticut State Department of Education for the current status of any choice or scholarship options.

What types of microschools are in Connecticut?

Both listed schools, The Dream Academy of CT and Sankofa Learning Center, use a Montessori approach. Montessori classrooms group mixed ages and let children move through hands-on materials at their own pace. Both programs are based in New Haven.

Where in Connecticut are the listed microschools located?

New Haven is the hub for microschools in this directory, since both current listings operate there. If you live elsewhere in the state, you may still find homeschool co-ops or pods that aren't listed yet, so it is worth asking around locally.
