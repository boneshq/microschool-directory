Microschooling & school choice in Colorado

Our directory covers 13 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 8 Colorado cities. Aurora leads with five listings and Denver follows with two, while single programs appear in places like Grand Junction, Colorado Springs, and Eagle. Tuition runs from $1,440 to $16,060 a year, a wide spread that reflects part-time co-ops on the low end and full-day private programs on the high end. Montessori is the most common approach with seven schools, followed by four learner-driven academies and two general microschools. Colorado has no statewide private-school-choice ESA or voucher, so families typically cover tuition out of pocket or through scholarships a school offers directly. Public options such as open enrollment and charter schools remain the main publicly funded alternatives.

Finding the right fit in Colorado

Start where the listings cluster: Aurora has five microschools and Denver has two, so families in the metro area have the most to compare side by side. Outside the front range, check single listings in Grand Junction, Colorado Springs, Littleton, and Eagle. Because Colorado has no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to pay tuition directly and ask each school whether it offers need-based aid or sliding-scale rates. On a visit, ask about the daily schedule, how many students share each guide, and whether the program runs full-time or as a part-time co-op. Highlands Micro School in Denver and The Sunflower School in Aurora are two listings worth touring to see how different models work in practice.

Colorado microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Colorado?

Our directory lists 13 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Colorado across 8 cities. Aurora has the most with five, and Denver has two. Smaller towns like Grand Junction and Eagle each have a single listing.

How much does a microschool cost in Colorado?

Tuition for Colorado microschools in our directory runs from $1,440 to $16,060 per year. The low end usually reflects part-time co-ops or limited schedules, while full-day private programs sit near the top. Ask each school what its fee covers before comparing prices.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Colorado?

Colorado does not run a statewide private-school-choice ESA or voucher, so most families pay microschool tuition directly. Some schools offer their own need-based aid or sliding-scale rates, so ask each one. Check the Colorado Department of Education for any current scholarship or tax-credit options.

What types of microschools are in Colorado?

Montessori is the most common model here with seven schools, followed by four learner-driven academies and two general microschools. Courage Academy in Colorado Springs runs an Acton-style learner-driven program, while The Farmstead School in Longmont follows a Montessori approach. Visiting a few shows how the formats differ day to day.

Are there microschools outside the Denver area in Colorado?

Listings reach beyond Denver into the western slope and mountain towns. Cactus Bloom Community School operates in Grand Junction, and Vail Valley Unbound serves the Eagle area. That spread means rural and resort-town families have options closer to home than they might expect.
