Microschooling & school choice in Atlanta

Seven microschools and homeschool co-ops in Atlanta appear in this directory. Listed tuition runs around $12,000 per year, though programs set their own rates and some come in lower. Three of the seven use a learner-driven model, two are project-based, and two describe themselves as general microschools. Georgia's school-choice picture changed in 2024 with the Promise Scholarship, an education savings account worth up to $6,500 per student that took effect for the 2025-26 year. Eligibility is income-based and tied to families zoned to lower-performing public schools, so check the program rules before counting on it.

Finding the right fit in Atlanta

Start by comparing the seven Atlanta listings side by side, since a learner-driven room like Acton Buckhead runs differently from a project-based program such as Legacy Learning Loft. Tuition sits near $12,000 a year at the listed level, so price out a couple of options against your budget. If money is a factor, look into the Georgia Promise Scholarship, which can put up to $6,500 per student toward private schooling for families who qualify on income and school-zoning rules. Apply through the state and confirm a given school accepts the funds before you commit. On a visit, ask about group size, the daily schedule, how guides track progress, and what a typical week looks like for a student your child's age.

Atlanta microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Atlanta?

This directory lists seven microschools and homeschool co-ops in Atlanta. The group leans toward learner-driven programs, with three of that type, plus two project-based schools and two general microschools. That gives parents a working range of formats to compare within the city.

How much does a microschool cost in Atlanta?

Listed tuition at Atlanta microschools sits around $12,000 per year. Individual schools set their own rates, and some charge less depending on grade level, days per week, and what the fee covers. Ask each program for its full fee schedule, including any registration or materials costs.

What learning models do Atlanta microschools use?

The seven listings split across three formats. Learner-driven schools, including Acton Buckhead and Acton Academy at Grant Park, let students set goals and work at their own pace. Two project-based programs build learning around hands-on work, and two run as general microschools with small mixed-age groups.

Can Atlanta families use Georgia's ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes. Georgia's Promise Scholarship took effect for the 2025-26 school year and provides up to $6,500 per student as an education savings account. It is income-limited and aimed at families zoned to lower-performing public schools, so confirm your eligibility with the state and check that the school accepts it.

How do I enroll my child in an Atlanta microschool?

To enroll, contact the schools you like directly, since most run their own application and rolling admissions rather than a shared system. Acton Buckhead and Future Lab School are two Atlanta listings worth a first call. Ask about open seats for your child's grade, start dates, and any waitlist before you visit.
