Microschooling & school choice in New Jersey

The directory currently covers 10 schools spread across 10 New Jersey cities, so most towns hold a single listing. Montessori is the dominant approach with five programs, including Honeypot Montessori in Newark and Birds of Paradise Montessori in East Orange. Four sites follow a learner-driven model, among them Acton Academy Bergen County in Closter and Acton Academy Essex County in Verona, and one is a general microschool. Each program sets its own tuition, so contact the school directly for current rates. New Jersey has no statewide ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship for private tuition; publicly funded alternatives are limited to charter, magnet, and inter-district public school choice.

Finding the right fit in New Jersey

Start your search in the cities with active listings: Newark and East Orange in Essex County, Closter and Verona toward Bergen, plus Bridgewater and Allenhurst. Because New Jersey offers no private-school ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship, plan to pay tuition out of pocket and check with your local district about charter, magnet, or inter-district options if you want a publicly funded route. Reach out to each school for its current rate, since none is fixed across the directory. On a visit, ask about student-to-teacher ratio, the daily schedule, how the school handles New Jersey homeschool reporting, and whether mixed-age grouping fits your child. It also helps to ask how a Montessori or Acton learner-driven classroom measures progress without traditional grades.

New Jersey microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in New Jersey?

This directory lists 10 microschools and homeschool co-ops in New Jersey, spread across 10 different cities. Most carry a single listing per town, from Newark and East Orange to Closter and Verona. Listings include Montessori programs and several Acton-style learner-driven academies.

How much does a microschool cost in New Jersey?

Tuition is not standardized in New Jersey, and this directory does not publish a fixed range. Each microschool and co-op sets its own rate based on its model, hours, and ages served, so request a current price sheet from the school. Programs such as Honeypot Montessori in Newark and Acton Academy Bergen County in Closter quote tuition individually.

Can you use a New Jersey ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

No. New Jersey does not run a private-school ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship program, so there is no state money to apply toward microschool or co-op tuition. Families pay out of pocket for these private options. If you want a publicly funded path, look instead at charter, magnet, and inter-district public school choice through your district.

What types of microschools are in New Jersey?

These listings split across three approaches: Montessori leads with five programs, learner-driven academies account for four, and one is a general-purpose microschool. Montessori sites include Dahlia Montessori in Allenhurst and Guidepost Montessori at Bridgewater. The learner-driven group is built around Acton Academy campuses in Bergen and Essex counties.

Do you have to register to homeschool in New Jersey?

Reporting rules matter in New Jersey. The state has no registration or notification requirement for homeschooling, but a microschool or co-op may still ask families to document instruction, so confirm how each program expects you to handle records before you enroll.
