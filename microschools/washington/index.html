Microschooling & school choice in Washington

The directory currently tracks 5 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 5 Washington cities: Kirkland, Federal Way, Renton, Buckley, and Castle Rock. Listed tuition starts around $1,200 per year, though costs vary by program and schedule. The model mix leans learner-driven, with two Acton-style learner-driven schools alongside one Montessori program, one project-based school, and one general microschool. Kirkland is home to The Kineo School, while Federal Way hosts a Guidepost Montessori campus. Washington does not offer a statewide private school choice program, so there is no ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship that covers private microschool tuition. Parents fund enrollment directly and should confirm current rates with each school.

Finding the right fit in Washington

If you live near Seattle, start with the Kirkland and Renton listings, then look south toward Federal Way and Buckley as you widen the search. Smaller towns like Castle Rock add a rural option if you are outside the metro. Because Washington has no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to pay tuition yourself; some individual schools offer sliding-scale rates or limited financial aid, so ask each one directly. On a visit, ask how many students share a classroom, how mixed-age grouping works, and what a typical day looks like. Request the full annual cost in writing, since the $1,200 starting figure may not include materials, field trips, or extended-day care. Confirm enrollment timelines early, as small programs often cap seats.

Washington microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Washington?

This directory currently lists 5 microschools and homeschool co-ops across Washington. They sit in five cities, including Kirkland, Federal Way, Renton, Buckley, and Castle Rock. The count reflects schools that have been verified for this listing, and it grows as new programs are added.

How much does a microschool cost in Washington?

Listed tuition for Washington microschools starts at roughly $1,200 per year. Actual prices depend on the program, the number of days per week, and whether care extends beyond the core school day. Always request the full annual figure from the school, since the starting rate may not include extras like materials or field trips.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Washington?

Washington does not run a statewide ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship for private tuition, so public funds cannot be applied to a private microschool here. Some schools offer their own sliding-scale pricing or limited financial aid, which you would arrange directly with them. Check with the school and the state education agency for any current eligibility before counting on outside funding.

What types of microschools are in Washington?

The five Washington listings span several models: two are learner-driven Acton-style schools, one is Montessori, one is project-based, and one is a general microschool. Acton Academy Bonney Lake in Buckley and Citizens Academy in Renton represent the learner-driven approach, while Guidepost Montessori in Federal Way runs a Montessori program. Visiting a few lets you compare how each handles mixed-age classrooms and student pacing.

Where are most Washington microschools located?

Most of these schools cluster around the Seattle metro, with The Kineo School in Kirkland and listings in Renton and Federal Way, plus options reaching south to Buckley and out to Castle Rock. That gives both suburban and more rural families a starting point. Because seats are limited at small programs, reach out early to confirm openings and tour dates.
