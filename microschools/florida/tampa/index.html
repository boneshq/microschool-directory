Microschooling & school choice in Tampa

Tampa has nine microschools and homeschool co-ops in this directory. Seven run a standard microschool model, while one uses a learner-driven approach and another is project-based. Listed tuition centers around $13,500 per year, though smaller co-ops and part-time programs often cost less, so check each school's own rate. Florida funds K-12 students through the Family Empowerment Scholarship and the newer Personalized Education Program, an education savings account for homeschooling families that covers tuition, tutoring, and approved materials. Many small Tampa programs accept these accounts, but acceptance varies by school.

Finding the right fit in Tampa

Start by comparing the nine Tampa listings side by side: note which use a microschool, learner-driven, or project-based structure, and which fit your child's age and schedule. Reach out to two or three that match, since most are small and fill seats fast. If you qualify for Florida's Personalized Education Program, the state deposits funds into an account you can spend on approved tuition and learning costs, so ask each school whether it accepts these accounts and how billing works. On a visit, ask about group size, the daily routine, how teachers track progress, and what a typical week looks like. Get tuition, fees, and the refund policy in writing before you commit.

Tampa microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Tampa?

This directory lists nine microschools and homeschool co-ops in Tampa. Seven follow a standard microschool model, one is learner-driven, and one is project-based. New programs open regularly across the Tampa Bay area, so the count can grow over time.

How much does a microschool cost in Tampa?

Listed tuition centers around $13,500 a year for a full-time microschool. Smaller co-ops, part-time options, and faith-based programs often cost less, so confirm the rate with each school. Several Tampa families offset tuition with a state-funded education account.

What models or types of microschools does Tampa have?

Most Tampa listings are microschools, where a small group of students learns together with one or two teachers. The directory also includes a learner-driven program, such as Apollo Academy, and a project-based one, Ingenue Preparatory Christian Academy. Each sets its own schedule, age range, and teaching method.

Can Tampa families use a Florida ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes, in many cases. Florida's Personalized Education Program is an education savings account for homeschooling families that can go toward tuition, tutoring, and approved materials, and the Family Empowerment Scholarship supports other K-12 students. Whether a given school accepts these funds depends on the school, so ask before you enroll.

How do I enroll, and what if Tampa schools are full?

Because seats are limited, contact schools like South Tampa Microschool or Primer Microschool directly to ask about openings and waitlists. If a Tampa program is full or not the right fit, nearby options in St. Petersburg, Brandon, and Clearwater are worth a look. Apply early, since many small programs enroll on a rolling basis.
