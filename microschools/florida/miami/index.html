Microschooling & school choice in Miami

Eight microschools and homeschool co-ops in Miami appear in this directory. Five run as microschools and three use a learner-driven model, including Acton Academy Miami South and The Miami Sudbury School. Sizes and daily schedules vary, so the range of options is wider than the model labels suggest. Florida runs the Family Empowerment Scholarship and the Personalized Education Program, two state-funded routes that families can put toward tuition, curriculum, or other approved costs. Because each program has its own eligibility and award rules, confirm the current amounts with Step Up For Students or the school before you commit.

Finding the right fit in Miami

Start by comparing the eight Miami listings on model, age range, and weekly schedule, since a microschool and a learner-driven program like Acton Academy Miami South run very differently day to day. Shortlist two or three that fit your child's age and your commute, then request a visit at each. Florida money flows through programs such as the Family Empowerment Scholarship and the Personalized Education Program, both administered by Step Up For Students, so check eligibility and apply early because awards are limited. On a visit, ask how many students share a room, who teaches each subject, how progress gets reported, and which scholarships the school already accepts. Also ask about start dates and waitlists, since smaller programs fill fast.

Miami microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Miami?

This directory lists eight microschools and homeschool co-ops in Miami. They span two main approaches, with five microschools and three learner-driven programs. That gives families a small but varied set to compare across the city and nearby Homestead.

How much does a microschool cost in Miami?

Pricing in Miami varies by program, so the most reliable way to learn cost is to contact each school directly. Rates differ by model, hours, and age group, and several programs accept state scholarship funds that can lower what a family pays out of pocket. Ask each school for its full-year rate and any sibling or part-time options.

What types of microschools does Miami have?

Miami's listings fall into two categories: microschools and learner-driven programs. Five are microschools, including Harmony and Health Microschool and The Paper Tree House. Three are learner-driven, such as Acton Academy Miami South and The Miami Sudbury School, where students set much of their own pace.

Can Miami families use a Florida ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes. Florida offers the Family Empowerment Scholarship and the Personalized Education Program, both run through Step Up For Students, and families can apply these toward tuition and approved educational costs. Eligibility and award amounts change year to year, so confirm your status with Step Up For Students and ask the school which programs it accepts.

How do I enroll my child in a Miami microschool?

To enroll, contact the Miami programs on your shortlist and ask about open seats for your child's grade. Some schools admit students year-round while others follow a set calendar, so check start dates and any waitlist. If a Miami school is full, the Homestead-based XILA Academy and other nearby options can widen your search.
