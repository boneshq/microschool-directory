Microschooling & school choice in South Carolina

Our directory tracks six microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across six South Carolina cities, including Greenville and Columbia. Three are Microschool programs, two follow a Project-Based approach, and one is Learner-Driven. Tuition is not published for these listings, so families should request current rates directly from each school. South Carolina families can apply to the Education Scholarship Trust Fund, the state ESA program, which directs public funds into accounts parents use for tuition and approved expenses. Eligibility and award amounts change year to year, so confirm the current rules with the state Department of Education before you count on that money.

Finding the right fit in South Carolina

Greenville and Columbia are good places to begin, since both anchor clusters of programs and sit near larger metro areas with more options. Smaller markets such as Florence, Taylors, and Moncks Corner each carry a listing worth a look if you live nearby. Check whether a school accepts the Education Scholarship Trust Fund, the South Carolina ESA, and how it handles billing against those accounts. Apply early, because award funding can be capped and rules shift between school years. On a visit, ask about group size, the daily schedule, how teachers track progress, and what a typical week looks like for a child your age.

South Carolina microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in South Carolina?

Our directory lists six microschools and homeschool co-ops in South Carolina, located in six different cities from Greenville to Florence. The directory grows as new programs are added, so check back for recent listings.

How much does a microschool cost in South Carolina?

Tuition is not posted for the South Carolina listings. Costs vary by program and schedule, so ask each school for its current rates and any sibling or part-time discounts.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a South Carolina microschool?

Possibly. South Carolina runs the Education Scholarship Trust Fund, an ESA that can put public money toward tuition and approved expenses. Confirm eligibility and whether a specific school accepts those funds before enrolling.

What types of microschools does South Carolina have?

The six listings split across three approaches: Microschool, Project-Based, and Learner-Driven. Acton Academy Greenville runs a Learner-Driven model, while Trinity Homeschool Academy in Columbia is Project-Based.

Are there microschools outside the major South Carolina cities?

Several listings sit near the coast and in the Lowcountry. A Home for School operates in Moncks Corner, and Lionsgate Academy serves Aiken County, so families outside the big metros still have nearby choices.
