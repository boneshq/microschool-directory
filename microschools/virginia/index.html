Microschooling & school choice in Virginia

This directory profiles 19 microschools and homeschool co-ops across 17 Virginia cities. Listed tuition centers around $9,600 a year, though individual programs set their own rates. The model mix leans toward learner-driven, project-based, and general microschool formats, with three Montessori programs and one classical school rounding out the options. Richmond has the most listings, followed by single programs in places like Williamsburg and Falls Church. For funding, Virginia runs the Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits Program, an income-limited tax-credit scholarship that can offset private education costs for qualifying families.

Finding the right fit in Virginia

Start in Richmond, where the directory shows the most options, then widen your search to nearby cities like Williamsburg or Falls Church if you need a closer fit. Virginia's Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits Program offers income-limited scholarships funded through donor tax credits, so check eligibility through a participating scholarship foundation before you assume a program is out of budget. Tuition for listed schools runs near $9,600 annually, but ask each school directly since rates vary. On a visit, ask how many students share a classroom, which learning model the school follows, and how teachers track academic progress. Confirm whether the school accepts scholarship funds and what its enrollment timeline looks like.

Virginia microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Virginia?

The directory lists 19 microschools and homeschool co-ops across Virginia. They span 17 cities, with Richmond holding the largest single cluster. Listings include programs such as Iron Village Preparatory Academy in Richmond and Vida Academy in Henrico.

How much does a microschool cost in Virginia?

Listed tuition centers around $9,600 per year. Individual schools set their own rates, so a program may cost more or less depending on grade level, hours, and services. Always confirm current pricing directly with the school.

Can you use a scholarship for a microschool in Virginia?

Possibly. Virginia runs the Education Improvement Scholarships Tax Credits Program, an income-limited scholarship funded by donor tax credits for families who meet the income threshold. Eligibility and accepted uses vary, so check with a participating scholarship foundation and the school before enrolling.

What types of microschools does Virginia have?

The listings cover learner-driven, project-based, and general microschool models, plus a few Montessori and one classical program. Northern Virginia Classical Academy in Fairfax is the classical option, while several project-based programs operate around Richmond and Norfolk.

Which Virginia cities have the most microschools?

Many microschools cluster in and around Richmond, which has the most listings in the directory. Hampton Roads cities such as Norfolk and Chesapeake also appear, alongside Northern Virginia spots like Fairfax and Falls Church. Solstice Hybrid Academy in Norfolk is one example outside the Richmond area.
