Microschooling & school choice in Tennessee

The 25 listings span 16 Tennessee cities, with the heaviest concentration in Nashville and Memphis (four programs each) and Knoxville (three). Tuition runs from $175 to $14,000 a year, a wide gap that reflects part-time co-ops at the low end and full-week academies at the high end. Project-based programs dominate with 13 listings, followed by six learner-driven schools, five labeled microschools, and one Montessori program. The state passed the Education Freedom Scholarship Act in 2025, creating a statewide scholarship that eligible families can put toward private and microschool tuition. Award amounts and eligibility shift year to year, so confirm current rules through the Tennessee Department of Education before you count on the money.

Finding the right fit in Tennessee

Start where the listings cluster: Nashville and Memphis each have four programs, and Knoxville adds three, so a parent in those metros has the most options to compare in person. Smaller hubs like Johnson City carry listings too, which helps if you want to stay outside the big cities. On the money side, Tennessee's Education Freedom Scholarship Act of 2025 set up a statewide scholarship that families can apply toward approved private and microschool costs; check the Tennessee Department of Education for current award amounts, deadlines, and which schools participate. When you visit a campus, ask how many students share each room, how the school handles different ages and grade levels, and whether it accepts the state scholarship. Also ask what a typical day looks like, since a $175 part-time co-op and a $14,000 full-week academy run very differently.

Tennessee microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Tennessee?

This directory lists 25 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Tennessee, spread across 16 cities. Nashville and Memphis lead with four programs each, and Knoxville has three. The rest are scattered through smaller cities like Johnson City, Spring Hill, and Cleveland.

How much does a microschool cost in Tennessee?

Listed tuition runs from $175 to $14,000 per year. The low end reflects part-time co-ops that meet a few days a week, while the upper figures come from full-week academies. Cost depends on how many days the school runs, the ages it serves, and what is bundled into the fee, so ask each program for an itemized number.

Can you use a Tennessee ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

Yes. Tennessee passed the Education Freedom Scholarship Act in 2025, which created a statewide scholarship families can apply toward approved private and microschool tuition. Award amounts, income rules, and participating schools change from year to year, so confirm the current terms with the Tennessee Department of Education and the school before enrolling.

What types of microschools are in Tennessee?

Project-based programs make up the largest share with 13 listings, followed by six learner-driven schools and five that describe themselves as microschools, plus one Montessori program. The Lab School of Memphis is one example of a microschool model, while Arbor Learning Lab in Murfreesboro runs a project-based program. Visiting two or three lets you see how the labels play out day to day.

Are there microschools in Tennessee outside Nashville and Memphis?

Several Tennessee listings sit outside the three biggest metros, including Hilton Horizons Academy in Johnson City and Friendship Academy in Columbia. If you live in a mid-size town, you may not have to drive to Nashville or Memphis to find a fit. Check the city pages in this directory to see what is listed near you before you widen the search.
