Microschooling & school choice in Michigan

These four programs sit in three Michigan cities, with Grand Rapids home to two of them, including Moving Mountains Montessori and Grove Montessori. The model mix runs to two Montessori schools, one learner-driven academy, and one general microschool, so parents can compare guided and self-directed approaches. Ada and Farmington Hills round out the list with one program each. Tuition is set school by school, and you will get accurate figures only by asking each provider. Michigan does not offer a statewide education savings account or private-school voucher, which keeps most of this cost on families. Some districts run schools-of-choice transfers and Section 23 nonpublic support that can affect specific programs, so confirm with each school what applies.

Finding the right fit in Michigan

Grand Rapids is the practical place to begin, since it holds two of the four listed programs and gives you a same-city comparison between Moving Mountains Montessori and Grove Montessori. Families near Detroit can look at AMPed Hybrid Academy in Farmington Hills, while Ada offers Journey Academy of Grand Rapids on the learner-driven side. Because Michigan runs no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to budget for tuition and ask each school about sliding-scale rates, sibling discounts, or payment plans. Check whether a program qualifies as a nonpublic school or operates as a homeschool co-op, since that changes your reporting duties to the state. On a visit, ask about student-to-teacher ratios, how mixed-age groups are run, and what a normal day looks like. Request references from current parents before you commit.

Michigan microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Michigan?

Four microschools and homeschool co-ops are listed in Michigan right now. They sit across three cities, with Grand Rapids holding two of them. The set covers Montessori, learner-driven, and general microschool models.

How much does a microschool cost in Michigan?

Tuition is set by each Michigan school rather than by a single statewide rate, so figures vary widely. This directory does not carry price tags for the four listings, which means you will need to ask each provider directly. When you call, ask about payment plans, sibling discounts, and any sliding-scale options.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship for a Michigan microschool?

Michigan has no statewide education savings account or private-school voucher, so there is no state ESA to spend on these programs. Most families pay tuition out of pocket or combine it with homeschool support and district options. Some districts offer schools-of-choice transfers or Section 23 nonpublic services, so ask each school what funding, if any, applies to their students.

What types of microschools are in Michigan?

The four listings break down into two Montessori schools, one learner-driven academy, and one program tagged as a general microschool. Montessori sites such as Moving Mountains Montessori and Grove Montessori in Grand Rapids lean on guided, hands-on materials. The learner-driven option, Journey Academy of Grand Rapids in Ada, gives students more say over their daily work.

Where in Michigan are these microschools located?

Geography matters in Michigan because the four programs cluster in the west and the Detroit metro rather than spreading evenly. Grand Rapids and nearby Ada hold three of the four, while Farmington Hills covers the southeast through AMPed Hybrid Academy. If you live outside those areas, expect a commute or ask schools whether they run any hybrid or remote days.
