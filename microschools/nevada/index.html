Microschooling & school choice in Nevada

The directory tracks five microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across four Nevada cities: Reno, Las Vegas, Elko, and Enterprise. Reno holds two of the listings, and the rest sit one per city. Three schools use a Learner-Driven approach, several of them Acton Academy campuses, and two are Montessori programs. Individual schools set their own tuition, so ask each one for a current figure rather than relying on a single statewide number. For funding, Nevada runs the Opportunity Scholarship, a tax-credit program that helps lower-income families pay private and microschool tuition; check the Nevada Department of Education for current eligibility and award amounts.

Finding the right fit in Nevada

Start in Reno, where two listings sit, or in Las Vegas if you are in the south. Both cities give you a Learner-Driven option such as an Acton Academy campus, and Reno also has a Montessori program. Reach out to each school for its tuition, since rates differ by program and grade. If money is tight, look into the Nevada Opportunity Scholarship, a tax-credit program for lower-income families, and confirm eligibility through the Nevada Department of Education. On a visit, ask about group size, the daily schedule, how the school handles different ages in one room, and what a typical week looks like for a student.

Nevada microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Nevada?

Five microschools and homeschool co-ops are listed in Nevada, spread across four cities. Reno has two, while Las Vegas, Elko, and Enterprise each have one. The mix leans toward Learner-Driven programs, with a few Montessori options.

How much does a microschool cost in Nevada?

Tuition is set by each school, so there is no single statewide rate. Most Nevada microschools fall in line with private-school pricing, but figures range widely by grade, hours, and program. The best move is to contact each school on your shortlist and ask for its current tuition and any sibling or scholarship discounts.

Can I use a scholarship or ESA in Nevada?

Nevada families can apply for the Opportunity Scholarship, a tax-credit program that funds private and microschool tuition for lower-income households. Award amounts and eligibility change year to year, so confirm the current rules with the Nevada Department of Education before you enroll.

What types of microschools are in Nevada?

Two main types show up in Nevada. Learner-Driven programs, including several Acton Academy campuses such as Acton Academy Red Rock in Las Vegas, let students set goals and work at their own pace. Montessori schools, like Desert Peach Montessori in Reno, use hands-on materials and mixed-age classrooms.

Which Nevada city has the most microschools?

Reno is the busiest spot for microschools in Nevada, with two listings, including Acton Academy Mt. Rose. Las Vegas and the nearby Enterprise area cover the south, so families in either metro have a starting point close to home.
