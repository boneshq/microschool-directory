Microschooling & school choice in California

The directory covers 29 microschools and homeschool co-ops spread across 26 California cities. Learner-driven programs make up the largest share at 16 listings, followed by 7 Montessori schools, 4 project-based programs, and 2 schools that describe themselves simply as microschools. Listed tuition sits at about $9,050 per year, though costs vary by program and schedule. San Francisco has the most options with four listings, and cities like San Jose and Hollister add to the spread. California does not run a statewide education savings account or voucher program, so most families pay tuition out of pocket and look for individual school payment plans or private scholarships.

Finding the right fit in California

Start your search in the cities with the most listings: San Francisco leads with four schools, and the Bay Area around San Jose and Hollister gives you more to compare. Decide early whether a learner-driven model, a Montessori classroom, or a project-based program fits your child, since those three account for nearly every listing in the state. California has no statewide ESA or voucher, so budget for paying tuition directly and ask each school about payment plans, sibling discounts, or private scholarships. On a visit, ask how many students share a teacher, how the school handles different ages in one room, and how it tracks academic progress. Confirm whether the school files as a private school or supports you in registering your own homeschool through a private school affidavit.

California microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in California?

Our directory lists 29 microschools and homeschool co-ops in California, spread across 26 cities. San Francisco has the most with four listings, and the rest are scattered through the Bay Area and Southern California. Khan Lab School in Mountain View and Little Tree Community in Hollister are two examples on the list.

How much does a microschool cost in California?

Listed tuition runs around $9,050 per year, but the figure varies by program, grade level, and how many days a week your child attends. Some schools charge less for part-time enrollment, and many offer payment plans or sibling discounts. Ask each school for its full fee schedule before you commit.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in California?

California does not have a statewide education savings account, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship program, so there is no public funding to apply toward these schools. Families generally pay tuition directly. Check your district and the California Department of Education for any current options, and ask schools about private scholarships.

What types of microschools are in California?

The listings span four main models: learner-driven programs are the most common with 16 schools, followed by 7 Montessori schools, 4 project-based programs, and 2 listed simply as microschools. Inner Fire Academy in San Francisco is one project-based example. Each model handles pace, age mixing, and assessment differently, so visit a few before deciding.

How are California microschools legally set up?

California families often run their microschool as a private school through a private school affidavit filed with the state. Many of the listed programs operate this way, while others enroll children as a small private school directly. Ask any school how it is legally structured and what that means for your reporting responsibilities as a parent.
