San Francisco microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in San Francisco?

This directory lists four microschools and homeschool co-ops in San Francisco. The group skews Montessori, with two of the four schools using that approach, and the remaining two running learner-driven and project-based programs. New listings get added over time, so it is worth checking back.

How much does a microschool cost in San Francisco?

Listed tuition for San Francisco microschools sits at $9,050 per year. That figure reflects the schools currently in the directory and does not include any aid a school might offer on its own. California has no statewide voucher or ESA, so families budget this cost directly and should ask each school about payment plans.

What learning models do San Francisco microschools use?

The four San Francisco schools cover three formats. Two are Montessori programs, including The Dahlia School of San Francisco, which lists both an elementary track and a broader program. The other two are The Neos Bayview Academy, a learner-driven school, and Inner Fire Academy, which is project-based.

Can San Francisco families use a California ESA or scholarship for a microschool?

California does not run an ESA, voucher, or tax-credit scholarship for private or home education in 2026. That means San Francisco families pay microschool tuition out of pocket rather than with public funds. If public money matters to your budget, charter and public-school options are the usual route, and your state education agency can confirm current rules.

How do I enroll my child in a San Francisco microschool?

Enrolling usually starts with a direct inquiry to the school, since microschools set their own admission timelines and class sizes. Reach out to schools such as The Dahlia School of San Francisco or Inner Fire Academy early, because small programs fill seats quickly. Ask about waitlists, start dates, and whether they accept students mid-year.
