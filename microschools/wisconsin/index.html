Microschooling & school choice in Wisconsin

The directory currently tracks 4 microschools and homeschool co-ops across 4 Wisconsin cities: Hudson, Oshkosh, Onalaska, and Spring Valley. Tuition listed for these schools ranges from $420 to $1,300 per year, which sits well below typical private-school pricing. Every listing uses a learner-driven approach, with three operating as Acton Academy campuses and one, Spring Forth Academy, in Spring Valley. Wisconsin also runs the Wisconsin Parental Choice Program, a statewide income-limited voucher, alongside separate choice programs in Milwaukee and Racine. Those vouchers apply to participating private schools, so families should confirm whether a given microschool meets the program's accreditation and enrollment rules.

Finding the right fit in Wisconsin

Start with the cities where listings already exist: Hudson and Oshkosh both have an Acton Academy campus, and Onalaska and Spring Valley round out the four. Reach out to each school directly to confirm grade levels, daily schedule, and current openings, since small programs fill quickly. On school-choice money, Wisconsin's Parental Choice Program offers income-limited vouchers for participating private schools statewide, with separate programs serving Milwaukee and Racine; check whether the microschool you like is enrolled and whether your household income qualifies. During a visit, ask how the learner-driven model works day to day, how progress gets measured without traditional grades, and what the adult-to-student ratio looks like. It also helps to ask about the typical age range in each mixed group and how new families are onboarded mid-year.

Wisconsin microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Wisconsin?

Our directory currently lists 4 microschools and homeschool co-ops in Wisconsin, located in Hudson, Oshkosh, Onalaska, and Spring Valley. All four use a learner-driven model. The list grows as new programs are verified and added.

How much does a microschool cost in Wisconsin?

Listed tuition for Wisconsin microschools runs from $420 to $1,300 per year. That range reflects part-time co-op pricing on the low end and fuller programs on the higher end. Costs vary by grade level, days per week, and whether meals or extras are included, so confirm the exact figure with each school.

Can you use an ESA or scholarship for a microschool in Wisconsin?

Possibly. Wisconsin's Parental Choice Program provides income-limited vouchers for participating private schools statewide, and Milwaukee and Racine run their own choice programs. A microschool has to be an enrolled, accredited private school for those vouchers to apply, and your household income has to meet the program limits. Ask the school whether it participates before counting on that funding.

What types of microschools are in Wisconsin?

Every Wisconsin listing right now uses a learner-driven model, where students set goals and move at their own pace rather than following a fixed class schedule. Three of the four are Acton Academy campuses, including Acton Academy Hudson and Acton Academy Oshkosh. The fourth, Spring Forth Academy, operates in Spring Valley.

Where in Wisconsin are these microschools located?

Most of Wisconsin's listed microschools sit in smaller communities rather than the Milwaukee or Madison metros. Acton Academy Hudson and Acton Midwest in Onalaska are examples in the western part of the state, near the Minnesota border. Families in larger cities may need to travel or consider a homeschool co-op while the directory expands.
