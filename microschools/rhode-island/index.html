Microschooling & school choice in Rhode Island

The directory currently shows two microschools across two Rhode Island cities, West Warwick and Providence. Both are Montessori programs, so the model mix here is uniform rather than varied. Neither school publishes a tuition figure, which is common for small independent programs that set rates by grade and schedule. Rhode Island does not run a statewide education savings account or voucher program, though a tax-credit scholarship exists for some lower-income families through participating organizations. Parents should confirm eligibility and current scholarship rules with the Rhode Island Department of Education before assuming aid applies.

Finding the right fit in Rhode Island

Start with the two cities that have listings: West Warwick and Providence. Orchid Montessori in West Warwick and Tiger Lily Montessori School in Providence both run Montessori classrooms, so it helps to read up on that approach before you call. Because Rhode Island has no statewide ESA or voucher, plan to pay tuition out of pocket and ask each school whether they accept the state's tax-credit scholarship or offer their own financial aid. On a visit, ask about class size, the mix of ages in each room, and a typical daily schedule. Request the full tuition and fee breakdown in writing, since neither listing posts a public rate.

Rhode Island microschool FAQ

How many microschools are in Rhode Island?

Two microschools are currently listed in Rhode Island. Both are Montessori programs, one in West Warwick and one in Providence. The directory will grow as more programs are added, so check back for new entries.

How much does a microschool cost in Rhode Island?

Neither Rhode Island listing posts a public tuition figure. Small Montessori programs usually set rates by age group and schedule, so you will need to contact each school for an exact number. Ask whether fees beyond base tuition apply.

Can I use an ESA or scholarship in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island does not offer a statewide education savings account or voucher in 2026. A tax-credit scholarship funded through participating organizations can help some lower-income families. Check current eligibility with the Rhode Island Department of Education and ask each school which aid it accepts.

What types of microschools are in Rhode Island?

Both listed schools use the Montessori model, which centers on mixed-age classrooms and self-directed work with hands-on materials. If you want a different approach, the directory may add other models as more Rhode Island programs join.

Where are Rhode Island's microschools located?

Orchid Montessori sits in West Warwick and Tiger Lily Montessori School is in Providence. Providence is the easiest hub to start from if you want to compare options in one trip. Driving between the two cities takes well under half an hour.
